How ZARIN Calculates Reputation

Every developer on this tracker carries a composite score on a 0-100 scale, built from four weighted indicators: on-time delivery (30%), construction quality (25%), complaint volume per 100 units (20%), and financial stability (25%). Inputs are pulled from Dubai Land Department transaction records, RERA project registrations and complaint filings, owner-submitted snagging reports (1,400+ entries across 38 developers), and sentiment analysis of 14 active owner communities. We deliberately weight on-time delivery highest because it is the most directly observable buyer cost — rent bridge, opportunity cost on capital, and disrupted life plans. We do not accept paid placements or developer-supplied scores; every input is external and verifiable. Scores recalculate on the first business day of each month; major regulatory actions trigger an out-of-cycle review within 24 hours, with corrections published transparently on this page. Full data sources, scoring weights, and update cadence are detailed in the methodology section below.

The four small cards on the right show the scoring weights applied to each indicator. The same weights apply to every developer on the tracker — we do not adjust weights per developer, per project class, or per marketing relationship. This is what makes the leaderboard comparable across developers of different sizes and specialisms.

Methodology, Part 1

Four Risk Indicators Explained

Every ZARIN developer score is built from the same four indicators, weighted to reflect what actually matters to off-plan buyers. Below we explain each indicator — what it measures, how it is calculated, and what thresholds trigger green / amber / red badges.

30% weight

On-Time Delivery

Measures whether projects are delivered by the contractual handover date specified in the Sales Purchase Agreement. This is the single most actionable metric for off-plan buyers — it tells you whether the date the developer quoted at booking is the date you will actually receive your keys.

Calculated from DLD Oqood-to-Title-Deed conversion timestamps against the original SPA handover date. Average delay figures are in calendar months. Green <2.0mo, Amber 2.0-3.0mo, Red >3.0mo. We weight this metric at 30% because direct financial cost of delay (rent bridge, opportunity cost on capital) is the most material buyer risk after outright default.

25% weight

Quality Rating

Letter-grade assessment of construction quality based on post-handover snagging reports, owner surveys, and independent building inspection data. Captures finishes, MEP reliability, common-area execution, and warranty responsiveness over the first 12 months post-handover.

Aggregated from 1,400+ owner-submitted snagging reports across 38 active developers, normalised by unit count to prevent scale bias. A+ requires <0.8 snags per unit and >90% defect-resolution rate within warranty. A rating reflects strong execution; B indicates average workmanship with recurring snag patterns; C and below warrant serious concern before committing capital.

20% weight

Complaint Volume

Normalised count of formal complaints per 100 delivered units, sourced from RERA complaint filings, consumer protection authority records, and verified owner community forums. Captures disputes over delay compensation, defect remediation, contract breaches, and misleading marketing claims.

We normalise by delivered units to avoid penalising larger developers for raw volume — what matters is the per-unit rate. A complaint index below 1.0 reflects market-leading customer resolution. 1.0-1.5 is acceptable industry standard. Above 2.0 indicates systemic disputes worth investigating before purchase. Above 3.0 triggers an automatic red flag in our scoring.

25% weight

Financial Stability

Composite assessment of escrow account discipline, project funding ratios, leverage, and where available, audited financials. Critical for off-plan buyers — developer default mid-construction is the worst-case scenario, leaving buyers with neither keys nor recovered capital.

Weighted 25% because the consequences of financial instability are catastrophic for buyers. We track RERA escrow compliance (the percentage of buyer funds held in regulated escrow vs released to developer), bond ratings where the parent is listed, and any project-cancellation history. Government-owned developers receive implicit sovereign backing, reflected in the rating. Independent developers face stricter scrutiny on funding ratios.

How to read the badges

Green (Low Risk) — meets or exceeds market-leading threshold. Amber (Medium) — acceptable but warrants a conversation with your advisor. Red (High) — serious concern; we would not recommend proceeding without significant due diligence. A developer with all four indicators green is rare; even top-ranked Emaar carries an amber on at least one metric in any given quarter.

Methodology, Part 2

Data Sources, Scoring & Disclaimer

ZARIN's Developer Reputation Tracker is built on six structured data streams. The Dubai Land Department (DLD) provides Oqood-to-Title-Deed conversion timestamps, which give us day-level precision on handover timing — the foundation of the on-time delivery metric. RERA provides project registrations, escrow account compliance status, and the formal complaint registry, which underpins both the complaint index and the financial stability indicator. For qualitative signals we monitor news archives in English, Arabic, and Farsi from 2014 onward, run sentiment analysis on 14 active owner-community forums (including Property Finder, Bayut, and Reddit r/dubai), and accept owner-submitted snagging reports — currently 1,400+ verified entries across 38 developers.

Scoring weights were calibrated by reviewing 12 months of post-purchase dispute data and 200+ buyer consultations. The weighting reflects what actually causes buyer pain: on-time delivery at 30% because direct delay cost (rent bridge, opportunity cost) is the most material buyer risk after outright default; construction quality at 25% because snag resolution consumes the first 12 months post-handover for many buyers; complaint volume at 20% as a leading indicator of systemic issues; and financial stability at 25% because the consequences of mid-construction developer default are catastrophic and largely unrecoverable.

Update cadence is layered. Active project status (handover dates, phase openings, cancellations) refreshes daily via RERA's project registry. Complaint and sentiment signals refresh weekly. The full composite score recalculates on the first business day of each month, with the historical trend chart rebuilt at the same cadence. Major regulatory actions — a developer entering default proceedings, a high-profile launch cancellation, an enforcement order from RERA — trigger an out-of-cycle manual review within 24 hours, and the score may be adjusted before the next monthly cycle.

Tracker FAQ

Eight Questions About the Tracker

The most common questions buyers ask about how the tracker works, what its scores mean, and how to use it in a real purchase decision. If your question isn't here, ask your ZARIN advisor — every consultation includes a walkthrough of any developer on the tracker.

How does ZARIN track developer reputation?
We aggregate six structured data streams — DLD transaction records, RERA project registrations, RERA complaint filings, news archives spanning 2014-2026, social media sentiment analysis, and owner-submitted snagging reports — into a weighted composite score. The methodology has been refined over 18 months of internal use and is reviewed quarterly by our advisory team. Each developer is scored on four indicators: on-time delivery (30%), construction quality (25%), complaint volume (20%), and financial stability (25%). The composite score is then ranked, with the top 10 published on this page. We deliberately use transparent weights rather than a black-box algorithm so buyers can understand exactly why a developer ranks where they do.

What data sources does the tracker use?
The primary structured source is the Dubai Land Department's transaction and Oqood-to-Title-Deed conversion records, which give us handover timing with day-level precision. RERA provides project registration data, escrow account compliance status, and formal complaint filings. For qualitative signals we use news archives (English, Arabic, and Farsi press), property forum sentiment analysis across 14 active owner communities, and a self-submitted snagging report dataset of 1,400+ entries from verified buyers. Financial stability inputs include audited statements where the parent company is listed, plus RERA escrow compliance ratios. We do not accept paid placements or developer-provided scores — all inputs are external and verifiable.

How often is the tracker updated?
Active project status (handover dates, phase openings) is refreshed daily via RERA's project registry API. Complaint and sentiment signals update weekly. The full composite score is recalculated on the first business day of each month, and historical trend charts are rebuilt at the same cadence. Major events — a developer entering default proceedings, a high-profile launch cancellation, or a regulator enforcement action — trigger an immediate manual review and may update the score out of cycle. The 'last updated' timestamp at the top of this page reflects the most recent full recalculation, not the daily status refresh.

Can I trust smaller developers with high scores?
A high score from a smaller developer should be read differently from a high score at scale. Sobha Realty, Select Group, and Ellington all post strong metrics — but their sample sizes are smaller, and a single late project can swing their numbers more than it would for Emaar or Nakheel. We flag this by including 'completed projects' count in the leaderboard; below 30 completed projects, treat the score as indicative rather than definitive. The financial stability indicator also matters more for smaller developers, who have less balance-sheet capacity to absorb a single project underperforming. ZARIN's advisory team is happy to walk through the specific sample-size context for any developer you're evaluating.

What is a good on-time delivery percentage?
Above 85% on-time delivery (within 30 days of the SPA handover date) is the threshold we consider market-leading. Emaar, Sobha, Meraas, and Ellington all sit above this line. Between 80% and 85% is acceptable but indicates recurring scheduling pressure — typical of large-volume developers like Nakheel or DAMAC. Below 80% warrants caution: buyers should expect a delay of 3+ months on average and budget for a rental bridge or extended interim accommodation. Anything below 75% is a red flag, suggesting either aggressive sales targets outpacing construction capacity or systemic project management weakness. We always read this metric alongside complaint volume, since the two often correlate.

How does ZARIN verify complaint data?
Complaint data comes from three verified sources: RERA's formal complaint registry (which is public record), the UAE Consumer Protection Agency's case filings, and our own curated dataset of owner-community forum reports. Forum reports are weighted lower than regulator filings because they cannot be independently verified — but they often surface issues 6-12 months before formal complaints are filed. We normalise all complaint counts by delivered units to avoid penalising larger developers for raw volume; what matters is the rate per 100 units, not the absolute number. Developers can dispute a complaint classification by submitting evidence to our advisory team, and we publish corrections when the dispute is upheld. We never accept developer funding in exchange for score adjustments.

Does the tracker cover all Dubai developers?
We currently track 38 active Dubai developers, which covers approximately 96% of all off-plan units sold in the past 24 months. The 10 displayed on the leaderboard are the highest-scoring; the remaining 28 are available on request through your ZARIN advisor. We do not track developers with fewer than 5 completed projects (statistically insufficient sample) or developers who have been inactive for 24+ months. Newer entrants — those with fewer than 3 delivered projects — are listed as 'under monitoring' until they accumulate enough delivery history to score reliably. If a developer you're considering is not in our tracker, ask your advisor and we will provide what data we can from public records.

Can I request a developer not listed?
Yes. Use the 'Speak to Analyst' button to request a custom report on any Dubai-licensed developer, including those not yet in our published tracker. We will compile what data is available from public RERA and DLD records, flag any red signals, and tell you frankly whether we have enough history to form a view. For developers with fewer than 5 completed projects, we provide a qualitative assessment instead of a numeric score — flagging escrow status, project registration history, ownership structure, and any complaint patterns. There is no charge for this service for clients actively evaluating an off-plan purchase. We aim to respond within 24 business hours with at minimum a preliminary view.
