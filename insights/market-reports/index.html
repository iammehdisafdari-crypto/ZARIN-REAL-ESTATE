Methodology

How the Numbers Are Built

Prices and volumes come from Dubai Land Department transaction records — registered transfers, not portal asking prices. Costs come from the RERA service-charge index, read at district and building-class level. Yields are computed on the ZARIN ROI basis: achieved rents against price, gross, on a consistent apartment base, with villa-dominant areas such as Emirates Hills shown at their villa base and flagged as trading 10–15% above the apartment benchmark.

The cadence is quarterly, on a fixed calendar: each edition publishes in the first weeks after the quarter closes. When a figure is restated — DLD revises late registrations, as registries do — the change is logged rather than silently absorbed, and methodology changes affecting the off-plan analysis are recorded in the investment guide changelog.

Where the report estimates rather than records — a handover window, a villa premium on a thin quarter of trades — the estimate is labelled in the annex, with its basis stated. The rule the founder has applied to his own portfolio since 2005 applies to the page: a number you cannot source is an opinion, and opinions are marked as such.

Reading the Data

Yield Buys Income. Price Buys Growth.

The single most useful idea in the eight-area table is the trade-off it encodes: high yield and strong appreciation rarely sit in the same district. Business Bay offers the table’s best gross yield at 7.0% on a AED 1,750/sqft base — income-led, cash-flowing, priced for rent. Jumeirah Bay Island sits at the other pole: AED 4,200/sqft at 4.2%, where the return arrives as scarcity and appreciation rather than monthly rent.

The Yield-Led Read

Business Bay at 7.0%, Dubai Marina at 6.4% on 1,850, Dubai Hills at 5.5% on 1,650. Entry tickets are lower, income is higher, and the daily data shows steadier pricing. The trade: service charges weigh more at these price points, so the net line deserves the service-charge arithmetic before you commit.

Suits: income investors, mortgage-funded buyers, first landlords.

The Appreciation-Led Read

Jumeirah Bay Island at 4,200/sqft and 4.2%, Palm Jumeirah at 2,800 and 4.9% — the quarters when these districts move, they move on scarcity, and Q3 2026 put Palm Jumeirah top of the appreciation table. The trade: income is thinner relative to capital, so timing and entry price carry more of the return.

Suits: long-horizon capital growth, trophy-asset mandates, refinancers.

Neither pole is “better” — the mix is the mandate

Most portfolios want some of both, weighted to the brief. The ROI Predictor runs the trade-off on live numbers, and the reports supply the quarterly evidence behind it — which is rather the point of publishing them.

Reports FAQ

Eight Questions About the Reports

Access, sources, coverage and permissions — the questions subscribers and journalists ask before almost anything else. If yours is not here, write to the desk.

Are the reports really free?
Yes — every report is free for registered briefing subscribers. The quarterly PDFs and the annual edition land in your inbox as they publish, with no paid tier, no gated “premium” summary and no drip-feed of partial data. We publish because the same numbers inform our advisory desk; the marginal cost of sharing them is zero and the trust they build is not. Register once via the briefing sign-up and the whole library follows.

How often are the reports published?
Quarterly, plus one annual edition. The rhythm is fixed: each quarterly lands in the first weeks after the quarter closes — Q3 2026 was published in October 2026 — and the annual consolidates the year each January. The Q4 2026 quarterly and the Annual 2026 edition are publishing January 2027, and subscribers receive both automatically.

What data sources do the reports use?
Dubai Land Department transaction records anchor every price and volume figure; the RERA service-charge index anchors the cost data; yields are computed on the ZARIN ROI basis and reconciled against achieved rents rather than asking rents. Where an estimate is used — a handover date, a villa premium — it is labelled as one in the methodology annex of each report.

Can I use the data in my own publication?
Yes, with attribution. Any quotation or chart should name ZARIN Real Estate and link to the relevant report page. For republication of full tables or sections in commercial materials — research notes, fund materials, press — write to advisory@zarinrealestate.com first; we almost always say yes, and we keep the underlying series available for verified media and research use.

Which areas do the reports cover?
Eight core districts in depth — Jumeirah Bay Island, Palm Jumeirah, Downtown Dubai, Dubai Creek Harbour, Emirates Hills, Dubai Marina, Business Bay and Dubai Hills Estate — plus the citywide aggregate. Apartment pricing is the base; villa-dominant areas such as Emirates Hills trade roughly 10–15% above it, and the report shows that premium explicitly rather than blending it away.

What is the difference between gross and net yield?
Gross yield is annual rent divided by price, before the bills. Net yield subtracts service charges, maintenance, management and voids — and it is the only yield that actually pays you. ZARIN quotes gross on a consistent ROI basis so districts compare cleanly, then shows the cost stack separately: service charges run from roughly AED 12/sqft/yr in Dubai Hills to roughly AED 22 in Downtown, which can move a net result by more than a full percentage point.

Do the reports cover off-plan?
Yes — developer performance tracking is a standing section of every quarterly, covering delivery track records, escrow discipline and handover quality. It is built on the same evidence base as the Developer Reputation Tracker, and the off-plan investment thesis behind it is documented in the investment guide.

How far back do ZARIN's reports go?
The published library runs from the Annual 2025 edition — 86 pages, published January 2026 — through the Q2 and Q3 2026 quarterlies. The analysis itself draws on much longer series: our founder has invested in this market since 2005, and the firm established in 2023 formalised the quarterly cadence so the record is public rather than private.

Between editions, the daily market data keeps the numbers current.
