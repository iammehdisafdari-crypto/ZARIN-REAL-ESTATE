Buying Advisory —
Measured Acquisition Counsel for Dubai Property

Buy-side representation across the entire Dubai market — structured around your mandate, disciplined through due diligence, and closed at the DLD trustee office in weeks, not months.

The Short Version

ZARIN acts as buy-side counsel for Dubai property acquisitions. The mandate comes first: budget, goal, financing and exit written down before a single viewing is booked. Filtering is unbiased across the whole market — resale listings from every brokerage, off-plan launches across developers, and private inventory that never reaches a portal — not just ZARIN's own stock. Every shortlisted asset then passes institutional due diligence: DLD title verification, developer escrow status, service-charge history, tenancies and liabilities. We negotiate and structure the offer, manage escrow, and complete the DLD transfer in roughly 30 days, including developer NOC and trustee appointment. Keys hand over to our after-sales custody team, not to a void. The commission is 2% of the purchase price, paid once at transfer per Dubai market convention, disclosed before the mandate begins. Our founder has invested in Dubai real estate since 2005; the firm was formally established in 2023.

Buy-Side Representation

What Buying Advisory Is

A listing agent is paid by the seller to move inventory. A buying advisor is retained by you — and answers to you alone. ZARIN's buying advisory is buy-side representation in the strict sense: we hold no stock to push, we take no developer placement incentives, and our shortlists are drawn from the entire Dubai market — resale listings from every brokerage, off-plan launches across developers, and private inventory that never reaches a portal. That fiduciary-style posture shapes everything downstream: the questions we ask, the flaws we surface, and the price we argue for.

In practice, the service is a sequence of written checkpoints rather than a stream of links. The mandate — budget, goal, timeline, financing, exit — is documented before the first viewing. Every shortlisted property carries a due diligence summary you can interrogate line by line. Every offer is drafted against live comparable transactions recorded at the DLD, not against the asking price. And every stage closes only when you approve it in writing. Buyers who have purchased in London, Singapore or New York tend to recognise the shape immediately: it is the buyer's-side counsel they assumed they already had.

The service scales across the whole market. It suits a first-time international buyer taking an AED 1M position in a Business Bay studio, a yield-focused investor assembling a portfolio against Dubai's citywide 6-7% gross, and a family acquiring an AED 80M trophy residence through our Private Office. Freehold ownership has been open to all nationalities in designated zones since the 2002 decree, and property above AED 2M qualifies for the 10-year Golden Visa — thresholds we plan around from the first mandate conversation.

The Process

The Five Buying Stages

From written mandate to title deed across five defined stages — each closing only when you approve its checkpoint in writing. Typical end-to-end duration: around 30 days including developer NOC and trustee appointment.

01

Understand Mandate

Before a single viewing, we document the mandate: budget and financing position, target yield or end-use, areas, timeline, and exit assumptions. The mandate is a written brief — the standard every later decision is measured against.

What you approve

the written mandate brief — budget, goal, areas, timeline, and how we will report to you.

02

Unbiased Filtering

We filter the entire market — portal inventory, broker-to-broker stock, off-plan launches and private networks — against the mandate, not against what ZARIN happens to hold. The output is a shortlist of five to eight properties, each with a one-page rationale and a first-pass price view.

What you approve

the shortlist before viewings are booked — and the viewing route itself.

03

Due Diligence

Every shortlisted property is checked against live DLD records: title, escrow status, service-charge history, tenancies, liabilities, and comparables. Snag audits are arranged for handovers. Findings land in a written summary — including the reasons to walk away.

What you approve

the due diligence summary, and the decision to proceed or walk.

04

Negotiate & Structure

We negotiate on evidence from recorded comparable transactions, and structure the offer — deposit size, payment schedule, completion conditions, and chattels — around your position. On off-plan, we work the payment plan; on resale, the Form F terms.

What you approve

every offer and amendment before it is tabled.

05

Conveyancing & Closing

We manage the escrow or trustee process, the NOC, mortgage registration, and the DLD appointment to title deed — typically around 30 days end-to-end including developer NOC. You attend, or we attend under power of attorney.

What you approve

the final settlement statement and the transfer appointment itself.

There is a sixth stage — and it never ends

The site-wide ZARIN process runs to six stages: the fifth delivers your title deed, and the sixth — Stay Supported (Custody) — begins the moment the keys change hands. Utilities, tenancies, yields and renewals stay under advisory long after closing.

Before You Sign

What Due Diligence Actually Checks

Eight checks run on every shortlisted property, all documented in a written summary before an offer is drafted. None of them rely on the seller's word; all of them rely on records — DLD title data, escrow registrations, service-charge ledgers, and Ejari tenancy files. If a check fails, you hear about it before money moves, not after.

Title Deed Verification at DLD

The property is checked against live Dubai Land Department records: confirmed ownership, mortgages, and encumbrances. No offer is drafted on a seller's claim alone.

Developer Escrow Account Status

For off-plan purchases, we verify the project's escrow account is registered and receiving payments as law requires — the single most important protection an off-plan buyer holds.

Three-Year Service Charge History

Service charges of AED 10-30 per square foot are the closest thing a Dubai building has to a medical record. Three years of history exposes leaky budgets and mismanaged boards.

Snag & Defect Audit

On handovers and near-new stock, an independent snag survey documents defects before money moves — and gives you the schedule needed to force developer rectification.

Existing Tenancy & Rental History

Rent rolls, Ejari registrations, and payment records establish what the asset actually earns — and which obligations transfer with it on day one.

Outstanding Liabilities & Mortgagor Consent

Unpaid service charges, fines, or a seller's mortgage all block transfer. We surface them early and structure the payoff into the completion statement.

Owners' Association Rules

Pet policy, short-let permissions, alteration rights, and parking allocations live in the OA declaration. They matter more to end-use value than most buyers assume.

Live Comparable Transactions

We price from recorded DLD transactions, not portal asking prices — the difference between negotiating on evidence and negotiating on advertising.

Who We Serve

Who This Service Suits

The process is constant; the weighting changes. Four buyer profiles, and what ZARIN adjusts for each — from a first AED 1M studio to an AED 80M trophy residence.

01

First-Time International Buyer

Often buying from abroad with no UAE footprint and no local intuition for which districts trade at which price per square foot. ZARIN adjusts by over-explaining: every stage carries a written summary, viewings are replaced with video walkthroughs and independent snag reports, and completion runs on power of attorney. The cost table is put in front of you before the first shortlist, so the AED figures behind a 4% DLD fee and 2% commission are never a surprise.

02

Yield-Focused Portfolio Investor

Measures everything against net yield and exit liquidity. Filtering is weighted accordingly — Business Bay at 6.8-7.5% gross and Dubai Marina at 6.4% screen differently from Palm Jumeirah at 4.9% — and due diligence leans hard on service-charge history, tenancy quality, and comparable exit transactions. The mandate defines the portfolio's target mix so each acquisition is judged as a position, not a one-off.

03

Relocating End-User

School runs, commuting, and community matter more than basis points. The lens shifts to end-use evidence: layout and storage, owners' association rules on pets and alterations, noise orientation, and the walk between the building and the school gate — checked on video when you cannot check it in person. Where the budget clears AED 2M, the Golden Visa pathway is planned into the purchase from the start.

04

Upgrading Within Dubai

Already knows the market, and is usually selling and buying simultaneously. ZARIN adjusts by managing the chain: timing the existing property's completion against the new purchase, modelling bridge positions if the dates do not align, and using the exit equity as negotiating leverage on the acquisition. Due diligence runs on the target while the sale is marketed — one team, both sides of your balance sheet.

Financing

Buying With a Mortgage as a Non-Resident

Dubai's banks lend to overseas buyers on documented income from any country — no UAE credit history or residency required. Non-resident financing typically runs at 50-65% loan-to-value on variable, EIBOR-linked rates of roughly 3.5-5.5%, with Emirates NBD, Mashreq and ADCB the most active lenders. Pre-approval is arranged before viewings begin, converting you into a credible buyer whose offer is treated like cash — typically within two to three weeks, and usually valid for 60 to 90 days.

On a AED 2,000,000 purchase at 60% LTV, the loan of AED 1,200,000 carries a mortgage registration fee of AED 3,000 (0.25% of loan value) and a trustee office fee of AED 5,000-5,500 — the only financing-specific additions to the cost table above. Our mortgage desk sequences the bank's valuation and the seller's NOC so neither stalls the other.

Buying FAQ

Eight Questions Buyers Ask First

Costs, timelines, remote completion, and what actually happens at the DLD trustee office — answered with numbers rather than adjectives. If your question is not here, ask us directly.

How much does buying advisory cost?
ZARIN's buying advisory costs 2% of the purchase price, paid once at transfer, per Dubai market convention — and the figure is disclosed in writing before the mandate begins, so it never changes mid-transaction. On an AED 2M purchase that is AED 40,000, covering mandate definition, market-wide filtering, due diligence, negotiation, and conveyancing through to the title deed. The other buyer-side costs sit outside our fee: the 4% DLD transfer fee, the AED 4,580 admin fee, and — where a mortgage is used — a trustee office fee of AED 5,000-5,500 plus mortgage registration at 0.25% of the loan. There are no retainer fees, no viewing charges, and no premiums layered on top.

How long does the Dubai buying process take?
A ready, secondary-market property typically completes in around 30 days from signed agreement to title deed, including the developer NOC and the trustee office appointment. The ZARIN side — mandate, filtering, and due diligence — usually takes one to two weeks before an offer is even drafted, because every shortlisted property is checked against live DLD records first. Financing runs on its own lane: non-resident mortgage approval commonly takes two to three weeks, which is why we arrange pre-approval before viewings. Off-plan purchases follow the developer's payment plan instead, with DLD registration at the first instalment. The honest variable is negotiation — a motivated seller closes in days; a stubborn one can add weeks. We will tell you which you are facing.

Can I buy Dubai property remotely without travelling?
Yes. Dubai's conveyancing system is built for it: the sale and purchase agreement, DLD transfer forms, and trustee office paperwork can all be executed under power of attorney, and ZARIN routinely completes purchases for overseas buyers who never set foot in the UAE. Viewings are replaced by video walkthroughs and independent snag reports; payments route through escrow or the trustee office, never to an individual. Non-resident mortgages are assessed remotely on income documents from any country — Emirates NBD, Mashreq, and ADCB all underwrite this way. The one caveat is documentary discipline: a POA must be notarised and, if issued abroad, attested for use in the UAE. Trusted legal partners manage that chain end-to-end.

What does due diligence actually cover?
Eight checks, documented in writing before you sign anything. Title deed verification against live DLD records to confirm the seller owns what is being sold. Developer escrow account status on off-plan purchases. A three-year service-charge history — the closest thing a Dubai building has to a medical record. A snag and defect audit on handovers. Existing tenancies and rental history. Outstanding liabilities and mortgagor consent. Owners' association rules, including pet and short-let policy. And live comparable transactions, so you negotiate on evidence rather than asking prices. On an AED 2M purchase, one avoided overpayment typically repays the advisory fee several times over.

Do I need mortgage pre-approval before viewing?
It is strongly recommended, and on ZARIN mandates it is standard practice before serious viewings begin. Pre-approval converts you from a browser into a credible buyer: sellers and their agents treat a pre-approved offer the way they treat a cash offer, which strengthens your position on both price and completion timeline. Practically, non-resident buyers can access 50-65% loan-to-value at variable EIBOR-linked rates of roughly 3.5-5.5%, with Emirates NBD, Mashreq, and ADCB the most active lenders. Income assessment is done remotely on documents from any country. Approval typically takes two to three weeks and usually remains valid for 60 to 90 days — long enough to complete a disciplined search without pressure.

What are the hidden costs of buying in Dubai?
There are few genuinely hidden costs — but several that first-time buyers forget to budget. The headline items are the 4% DLD transfer fee and the 2% agency commission paid by the buyer per Dubai market convention. Below those sit the AED 4,580 DLD admin fee and, on mortgage purchases, a trustee office fee of AED 5,000-5,500 plus mortgage registration at 0.25% of the loan value. On an AED 2M cash purchase, total transaction costs come to roughly AED 124,580 — about 6.2%; at 60% loan-to-value, roughly AED 132,830 — about 6.6%. Ongoing, service charges typically run AED 10-30 per square foot annually. Every figure is put in writing before you commit.

Can ZARIN represent me at the DLD transfer?
Yes — that is stage five of the process, and it is included in the 2% advisory fee. ZARIN attends the Dubai Land Department or trustee office appointment, manages the paperwork sequence — NOC, mortgage release or registration, clearance certificates — and executes the transfer on your behalf under power of attorney if you are abroad. Our conveyancing is trilingual — English, Arabic, and Farsi — which matters because DLD documentation and developer correspondence frequently run in Arabic. The transfer itself is administrative rather than dramatic: funds are exchanged through the trustee office, the deed is reissued in your name, and the appointment typically takes under two hours. You receive the scanned title deed the same day.

What happens after I get the keys?
The mandate does not end at handover — it moves into stage six: Stay Supported. ZARIN's after-sales team takes custody of the practical items: DEWA and district-cooling activation, service-charge registration, snag-list follow-up with the developer, and — if the property is an investment — tenant sourcing, rent collection, and annual yield optimisation. For investors targeting residency, we track the AED 2M Golden Visa threshold documentation and its filing. For overseas owners, we act as your local presence: inspections, renewals, and the small failures that become large ones when nobody is watching. The founder has run his own portfolio this way since 2005; the firm was formally established in 2023 to offer that discipline to clients.
