The Short Version

The four facts that head most conversations: buying costs in Dubai are 4% DLD transfer fee plus 2% buyer-paid commission plus AED 4,580 admin — AED 124,580 all-in on an AED 2M cash purchase (6.2%), or AED 132,830 financed at 60% LTV (6.6%). Foreigners of any nationality can buy freehold, with transfers taking roughly 30 days. Property from AED 2M earns a 10-year Golden Visa (mortgaged allowed if the unpaid balance is no more than 50% of title deed value), and the two-year Investor Visa starts at AED 750K. And the market itself, per DLD Q3 2026: AED 1,650 per sqft citywide, up 12% year on year, on 38,247 transactions. Our founder has fielded these questions with his own money at stake since 2005; the firm was established in 2023 to answer them for clients with the same arithmetic. The thirty-seven below are organised by topic — start where your question starts.

Topic 3 of 06

Buying & Costs

Fees, timelines, eligibility and finance — the seven questions that decide whether a Dubai purchase adds up before a single viewing is booked. Every figure here is the canonical one our advisors quote in consultations.

How much commission does a buyer pay in Dubai?
ZARIN’s commission on a purchase is 2% of the purchase price, buyer-paid per Dubai market convention — AED 40,000 on an AED 2,000,000 property, itemised in writing before you commit. It is the only fee ZARIN earns on the purchase; there is no markup on anything downstream, and the buying advisory page shows exactly where it sits in the total.

What taxes and fees does a buyer actually pay?
Three lines, no surprises: the DLD transfer fee of 4% of the price, ZARIN’s 2% commission, and a DLD admin fee of AED 4,580 covering title issuance and registration. Dubai levies no annual property tax and no capital gains tax on resale. On an AED 2,000,000 purchase that totals AED 124,580 — 6.2% all-in, cash.

How long does a property transfer take?
Roughly 30 days from signed MOU (Form F) to title deed on a straightforward cash resale — the developer’s no-objection certificate, clearance of any existing mortgage and trustee-office scheduling account for most of the calendar. Financed purchases add the bank’s valuation and approval steps on top. ZARIN’s conveyancing desk runs the sequence against that calendar rather than letting it drift.

Can foreigners buy property in Dubai?
Yes — in designated freehold areas, buyers of any nationality own apartments and villas outright, with full title deeds recorded at the DLD and the same resale and inheritance rights as any owner. Leasehold and usufruct structures exist for other zones. Every district ZARIN covers — from Business Bay to Palm Jumeirah — is freehold territory.

Can I buy under a power of attorney?
Yes. A notarised power of attorney lets your representative sign the MOU, attend the transfer appointment and collect the keys; a POA issued abroad must be notarised and attested for use in the UAE. Most of our overseas buyers never fly in — viewings run on video and the POA does the signing, with the after-sales desk holding the file remotely afterwards.

Can non-residents get a Dubai mortgage?
Yes — non-resident lending typically runs at 50–60% loan-to-value through partner banks, with income documented from abroad and the property as security. The mortgage calculator models the monthly figure before you shop; for live terms, our desk coordinates introductions to the banks active in non-resident lending. Mortgage registration adds 0.25% of the loan at the DLD.

What does an AED 2,000,000 purchase cost end-to-end?
In cash: AED 80,000 DLD transfer fee (4%), AED 40,000 commission (2%) and AED 4,580 in admin fees — AED 124,580, or 6.2% of the price. Financed at 60% loan-to-value, the mortgage registration fee (0.25% of the loan, AED 3,000) and the bank’s valuation and processing costs lift the total to about AED 132,830 — 6.6%. Every line of both columns is reconstructible from the price alone, which is rather the point.
Topic 4 of 06

Off-Plan Questions

Escrow, Oqood, payment structures and resale before completion — the machinery that stands between an off-plan buyer and a finished building, explained without the brochure’s optimism.

What is an escrow account and why does it matter?
The vault your money sits in. On an off-plan purchase, instalments are paid into a project-specific escrow account supervised by the DLD, and funds are released to the developer only against verified construction milestones — never on request. It exists so a stalled project cannot quietly spend your balance, and ZARIN confirms the escrow status of every project before a client commits a dirham.

What is Oqood?
The DLD’s interim registration for off-plan sales. Oqood records your buyer’s interest on the official register from the first instalment — before the building exists and the title deed can issue. It is what turns an off-plan contract into a registered property right rather than a promise on paper, and it is what an assignment later transfers.

How do off-plan payment plans work?
Instalments follow construction: a down payment at booking, a schedule of milestone-linked payments through the build, and the balance at handover. A 60/40 plan, for example, collects 60% across construction milestones and 40% on key collection; post-handover variants stretch part of the balance over years after you move in. The payment plan calculator maps any structure date by date.

Is buying off-plan riskier than buying ready?
Completion risk is the honest answer: you are buying a promise with a date on it. The structural mitigations are DLD-supervised escrow, Oqood registration and milestone-linked payments; the behavioural one is developer due diligence before you commit. ZARIN rates delivery track records publicly in the developer reputation tracker, because a schedule is only as good as the builder behind it.

Can I resell an off-plan property before completion?
Yes — by assignment. Your Oqood-registered interest is transferred to a new buyer with the developer’s no-objection certificate, typically for an NOC fee of AED 500–5,000 depending on the developer. The premium or discount to your original price is yours to negotiate, and the Off-Plan Resale Assessment inside the free Market Price Assessment prices the position properly before you list it.

Where should I go deeper on off-plan investment?
Three places, in order: the off-plan investment guide — the canonical walkthrough of structures, risks and the quarterly changelog of our lookup tables; the developer reputation tracker for who actually delivers; and the ROI predictor for the yield mathematics on a specific launch. Between them they cover more than any brochure will volunteer.
Topic 5 of 06

Visas & Residency

What property buys beyond the property itself: the 10-year Golden Visa at AED 2M, the 2-year Investor Visa at AED 750K, and the routes that bridge the gap. Thresholds are the current canonical figures.

What is the Golden Visa property threshold?
AED 2,000,000 minimum in Dubai property earns the 10-year Golden Visa, with processing typically taking two to three months and full family sponsorship included. The threshold is measured on property value — a single asset or a portfolio meeting it jointly. It is the strongest residency instrument Dubai property buys.

Can I get a Golden Visa on a mortgaged property?
Yes — provided the unpaid mortgage balance does not exceed 50% of the property’s title deed value. The equity you have actually built is what counts, not the headline purchase price. The bank and the DLD evidence the balance at filing, and our desk assembles that statement alongside the usual title documentation.

What is the AED 750,000 Investor Visa?
The two-year renewable investor residency, available from AED 750,000 in property — the pragmatic route when the budget does not reach the Golden threshold but residency is still the goal. It keeps you, your tenancy registrations and your utility accounts in the system while the portfolio grows toward the AED 2M upgrade.

Is there a retirement visa?
Yes — Dubai offers a five-year retirement visa for those who meet the property and financial conditions attached to the route; property is one of the qualifying bases. The five-year term makes it the longest standard route after the Golden Visa, and the visa page carries the current conditions and the filing sequence.

What about the remote work visa?
The one-year remote work visa lets you live in Dubai while employed by an employer abroad — income evidence, not property, is the basis. For buyers it is often the bridge: residency now, while the property hunt or a Golden Visa filing runs its two-to-three-month course. It renews, and it does not require you to buy.

Which visa would I actually qualify for?
That is precisely what the seven-question eligibility checker answers — budget, property status, basis and intent, mapped instantly to the Golden, Investor, Retirement and Remote Work routes. For anything the checker flags as marginal, a consultation settles it with the actual rules rather than the folklore.
Topic 6 of 06

Areas & Yields

Where the money goes and what it earns. Six questions on the spread between Dubai’s districts — with the full eight-area benchmark table reproduced once, below the answers, for reference.

Which Dubai area offers the highest gross yield?
Business Bay, on the ZARIN 2026 basis: 7.0% gross at AED 1,750 per sqft. Dubai Marina follows at 6.4% and Downtown Dubai at 6.0%. The full eight-area table sits just below — but note the companion question: yield is what you are paid for holding; appreciation is what you are paid for choosing.

Where are Dubai prices highest?
Jumeirah Bay Island, at an average AED 4,200 per sqft — the trophy district, trading at more than double the citywide average of AED 1,650. Palm Jumeirah follows at AED 2,800 per sqft. These are apartment-base figures; the island’s villas trade further above them still.

Should I chase yield or appreciation?
Know which one you are buying. Business Bay and Marina pay you now (7.0% and 6.4% gross); Palm Jumeirah and Jumeirah Bay Island pay you later (4.9% and 4.2% gross, with the stronger capital growth — the Palm led appreciation citywide in Q3 2026). Neither choice is wrong; confusing the two is expensive. The honest planning question is which decade you are optimising for.

How much of a premium do villas command?
In villa-dominant areas, villas and townhouses typically trade 10–15% above the apartment base on a per-sqft basis — and Emirates Hills, where the AED 1,900 figure is itself a villa base, shows how the premium compounds with land. The premium buys land, privacy and scarcity; the yield column shows what it costs, with Emirates Hills at 3.8% gross.

What is a realistic average for Dubai overall?
AED 1,650 per sqft citywide with an average gross yield of about 6.5%, per DLD data for Q3 2026 — a blend across everything from Business Bay studios to Palm penthouses. It is a health indicator for the market, not a price for any specific property; for a property-specific figure, the free Market Price Assessment exists.

Where can I compare areas in detail?
The areas hub carries the full set of district pages — Dubai Marina, Palm Jumeirah, Downtown Dubai, Business Bay, Dubai Hills Estate, Creek Harbour, Jumeirah Bay Island and Emirates Hills — each with the local price basis, yields and stock character. Pair them with the market data dashboard for the current quarter’s numbers.
Topic 7 of 06

ZARIN Services

What the firm does, what it deliberately does not do, and what it coordinates through trusted partners instead. Scope stated plainly is the whole philosophy — the services hub carries the full detail behind each answer.

What is the Market Price Assessment?
ZARIN’s free valuation service — an advisory indicative market appraisal, not a formal valuation, and it does not replace a bank or DLD valuation. It returns five written deliverables: a Selling Price Recommendation, a Rental Price Assessment, a Current Market Competition Analysis, a Property Selling Strategy and an Off-Plan Resale Assessment. Free, and precise about what an appraisal can and cannot promise.

How much does ZARIN charge?
On purchases: 2% of the price, buyer-paid, per Dubai market convention — nothing hidden downstream. Selling mandates, management and coordination work are quoted per scope, in writing, before work begins. There is no markup on contractor or supplier invoices anywhere in the business; the fee you sign is the fee you pay.

What happens after I get the keys?
Ownership begins and the mandate continues. The after-sales desk runs the first thirty days — DEWA and district-cooling connections, Ejari registration if you are letting, insurance placement and the snagging audit while the developer’s one-year defect window is open — then holds the file: tenant sourcing and vetting where commissioned, maintenance coordination at cost, and an annual written review against area benchmarks.

Can ZARIN design and furnish my property?
We coordinate; we do not design. ZARIN does not employ interior designers — furnishing and interior work runs through a tender of vetted designers and contractors, held to a timeline with quality checkpoints, with every supplier invoice passed through at cost and every spend approved by you before it is committed. The creative work belongs to the specialists you appoint from that tender.

Does ZARIN have in-house mortgage, legal and insurance teams?
No — and we are precise about it. Mortgage, legal and insurance work is coordinated through trusted partners: partner banks for lending, qualified legal firms for conveyancing and disputes, UAE-licensed insurers for cover. ZARIN manages the interfaces and the accountability; the regulated specialists do the regulated work. Anyone describing all of that as “in-house” is blurring lines we keep sharp.

Can ZARIN manage a property if I live abroad?
Most of our files are owned from abroad. Video inspections, notarised power of attorney for what needs signing, statements timed to your time zone, and rent reconciled and redeposited wherever you bank. The advisor holding your file is the one who ran your purchase — which is the point of custody rather than a call-centre rotation.
Topic 8 of 06

Market Data

Sources, cadence and the honest limits of averages. These six answers cover everything the market data dashboard publishes — and the one thing it deliberately does not claim to be.

Where does ZARIN's market data come from?
Two sources, both public and authoritative: DLD transaction records — what actually transferred, at what price, recorded by the land registry — and the RERA service-charge index for annual ownership costs. We add no proprietary blending; the numbers on the dashboard are traceable to their source lines, and the quarterly changelog records every basis change.

How often is the data updated?
Every 24 hours. DLD transaction records refresh daily on the market data dashboard; the deeper lookup tables behind the calculators are reviewed quarterly on the ZARIN 2026 basis, each review logged in the investment guide’s changelog. Daily for the pulse, quarterly for the diagnosis.

What is the current state of the Dubai market?
Q3 2026, per DLD: a citywide average of AED 1,650 per sqft, up 12% year on year, on 38,247 transactions in the quarter, with an average gross yield of about 6.5%. Business Bay leads the yield table at 7.0% gross; Palm Jumeirah leads appreciation. The dashboard carries the area-level detail behind each of those numbers.

How is net yield different from gross yield?
Gross is rent over price; net subtracts the costs of holding. The canonical example: a 1,000 sqft Dubai Marina apartment at AED 1,850,000 renting near AED 118,400 is 6.4% gross — but roughly AED 16,000 in service charges takes the net income to about AED 102,400, or 5.5% net. A quote that shows only the gross line is selling you half the arithmetic.

Which area led appreciation in Q3 2026?
Palm Jumeirah — the top appreciation district of the quarter on the DLD’s Q3 2026 records, at an average AED 2,800 per sqft. It is the same trade the yield table shows from the other side: the Palm pays 4.9% gross while it compounds; Business Bay pays 7.0% gross while it does not.

Is a citywide average useful for valuing my property?
As context, yes; as a valuation, no. The AED 1,650 figure blends prime and mainstream stock into one number. For a property-specific figure, the free Market Price Assessment returns an indicative appraisal built on your unit’s own comparables — advisory guidance, not a bank or DLD valuation.
