The Short Version

ZARIN, established in 2023 on two decades of Dubai deal-making, publishes six buyer’s guides that compress the desk’s working knowledge: the six-stage process from mandate to post-handover support; remote purchase by power of attorney, escrow and remote closing; off-plan payment plans, Oqood and handover; the full buyer cost stack — DLD 4%, commission 2% and AED 4,580 admin, AED 124,580 on a AED 2M cash purchase (6.2%); visa thresholds from the AED 2M Golden Visa to the AED 750K Investor Visa; and the landlord’s arithmetic across eight districts, from Business Bay at 7.0% gross to Jumeirah Bay Island at 4.2%. Our founder has traded this market since 2005; these guides are how the desk shares the method.

Guide 1

The First-Time Buyer Path

The first guide is the process itself. Every ZARIN buying mandate runs the same six stages — the sequence is the discipline, and the discipline is the product. Nothing here is theory: it is the order the desk runs for every client, and the order our founder applied to his own two project-level investments in this market since 2005.

Read it once before the first viewing and once after the shortlist lands. The stages are numbered because they compound: a mandate written properly makes the filtering honest, the filtering makes the due diligence short, and the due diligence is what gives the negotiation its teeth.

01 Stage

Understand Mandate

Budget, financing, purpose, districts and horizon are written into a mandate before a single viewing is booked. A precise brief is what makes the five steps after it fast.

02 Stage

Unbiased Filtering

ZARIN holds no developer inventory and sells nothing of its own, so the shortlist is filtered from the whole market — ready and off-plan — against the brief alone.

03 Stage

Due Diligence

Title status, service charges, comparable transactions and, for off-plan, escrow standing and the developer’s delivery record are checked before any money moves.

04 Stage

Negotiate & Structure

Price, payment terms and financing are negotiated together, in writing — with mortgage options from partner banks compared side by side rather than bolted on afterwards.

05 Stage

Conveyancing & Closing

The transfer is prepared, the DLD 4% transfer fee and AED 4,580 in admin are settled, and registration typically completes within about 30 days of documents being in order.

06 Stage

Stay Supported

Handover is not the end: utilities, service charges, tenant sourcing and furnishing coordination continue through After-Sales, so the property performs from month one.

Guide 2

The Non-Resident Buyer

Dubai places no residency requirement on property ownership: a buyer abroad can research, contract, fund and transfer without a single flight. What the distance demands instead is paperwork discipline — and this guide is that discipline, in order.

Four steps carry a remote purchase: the power of attorney that authorises, the banking that funds, the escrow that protects, and the remote closing that completes. The desk runs the India, UK, China and Iran/GCC country desks for exactly this workflow, in English, Arabic and Farsi.

The long-form version — jurisdiction by jurisdiction, with the attestation chains spelled out — is the article length treatment on the blog.

Read the Non-Resident Buyer’s Blueprint
STEP 01

Power of Attorney

A properly notarised and UAE-attested power of attorney appoints your representative to sign, transact and complete the transfer in your name — the single document the whole remote purchase turns on.

STEP 02

Banking & Funds

A UAE account and a clean funds trail are arranged before the offer, not after it. Buyers working across currencies use the desk’s multi-currency service, and the India, UK, China and Iran/GCC desks handle the paperwork at both ends.

STEP 03

Escrow Discipline

Ready-property payments move through regulated channels; off-plan payments go only to the project’s DLD-supervised escrow account — never to a developer’s current account.

STEP 04

Remote Closing

The transfer completes at the registration trustee with the POA in force, the title deed issues, and keys or handover follow. Remote closings are routine business for this desk.

Guide 3

The Off-Plan Investor

Off-plan is where payment structure does the heavy lifting: capital is staged against construction, and the discount to completed stock is the compensation for time and risk taken early. Guide 3 keeps that discipline mechanical — four mechanisms, in the order you meet them.

Two tools do the arithmetic for you: the investment guide carries the full thesis and the changelog behind it, and the payment calculator turns any developer plan into a month-by-month cash-flow table before you sign a reservation.

The Off-Plan Investment Guide → The Payment Plan Calculator → The Developer Reputation Tracker →
01

Payment Plans

Construction-linked plans — 60/40 and 80/20 are the common shapes — stage your capital against build progress, while post-handover plans defer part of the price beyond completion. Model any plan against your own cash flow before reserving.

02

Escrow Accounts

Every dirham paid on an off-plan unit must land in the project’s DLD-supervised escrow account, released only against verified construction milestones. Escrow is the structural protection — confirm it exists before the first payment.

03

Oqood Registration

Oqood is the DLD’s interim registration that records the off-plan unit in your name. It is your proof of ownership until the title deed replaces it at handover — insist on it, in writing.

04

Handover

At completion comes snagging, service-charge setup, the Oqood converting into a title deed, and the decision: move in, or go straight to tenant. The Developer Reputation Tracker grades how developers actually behave at exactly this step.

Guide 4

Costs & Fees: The Full Stack

Most cost confusion in Dubai comes from mixing the one-time with the recurring. Guide 4 separates them. At transfer you pay three items: the DLD transfer fee of 4% of the purchase price, ZARIN’s commission of 2% (buyer-paid, due on transfer), and AED 4,580 in admin and registration — with the transfer itself typically completing within about 30 days.

Every year after that you carry service charges (set by district, indexed by RERA), insurance of AED 1,500–3,500 a year, and — if the unit is let — the costs of keeping it lettable. A one-time furnishing budget of AED 50,000–150,000 covers most apartments the desk coordinates.

Guide 5

Visas & Residency

Property and residency are separate questions in the UAE — ownership alone confers no residency — but property is the most common route to both. Guide 5 is the threshold ladder, rung by rung, so the visa decision can inform the budget rather than trail it.

The single most consequential line: a mortgaged property qualifies for the Golden Visa only while the unpaid balance stays at or below 50% of the title deed value. Buyers planning a large loan alongside a visa objective need that arithmetic before the reservation, not after.

Golden Visa

10 years
Threshold: AED 2M

The flagship property route: full family sponsorship, 2–3 months processing, and a mortgaged property qualifies only while the unpaid balance stays at or below 50% of the title deed value.

Investor Visa

2 years
Threshold: AED 750K

The entry threshold for property-linked residency — less than half the Golden Visa ticket, renewed on the same property.

Retirement Visa

5 years
Threshold: Age 55+

For buyers structuring later-life residency around Dubai property, with financial criteria set by the retirement route rather than a single property threshold.

Remote Work Visa

1 year
Threshold: Employment abroad

For buyers who keep their job — and their income — outside the UAE while living in the property they buy.

Thresholds are floors, not verdicts

The route that fits depends on the property, the financing and the family plan together. Run the exact profile through the checker before you reserve anything.

Guide 6

The Landlord

The sixth guide is for the buyer whose property has to work from month one. It starts with the honest yield table — the same locked figures behind the market data dashboard — because the district choice sets the ceiling on everything else.

Then the deductions: service charges from roughly AED 12/sqft/yr in Dubai Hills to roughly AED 22 in Downtown, insurance, and the voids between tenants. A 6.4% gross in Dubai Marina is closer to 5.5% net once the annual charges are paid — a discipline the founder has applied to his own holdings since 2005.

What ZARIN does not do is design interiors: the desk coordinates furnishing — procurement, logistics, installation through trusted partners — and it sources and vets tenants, but the design decisions stay yours.

Guides FAQ

Eight Questions Buyers Ask First

Costs, distance, protection, visas and yields — the questions that arrive before almost any viewing. If yours is not here, write to the desk.

Which guide should a first-time buyer start with?
Guide 1, the First-Time Buyer Path, because it sequences everything else: the six-stage process from mandate to post-handover support. Read Costs & Fees alongside it — most first-time surprises live in the cost stack, not the property.

What does buying in Dubai actually cost?
At transfer: the DLD fee of 4% of the purchase price, ZARIN’s commission of 2% (buyer-paid), and AED 4,580 in admin and registration. On a AED 2M cash purchase that totals AED 124,580 — 6.2%; the same purchase with a 60% LTV mortgage totals AED 132,830, or 6.6%. Then budget recurring service charges, from roughly AED 12/sqft/yr in Dubai Hills to roughly AED 22 in Downtown, plus insurance of AED 1,500–3,500 a year.

Can I really buy without visiting Dubai?
Yes — residency is not a requirement of ownership, and remote purchases are routine for the desk. A notarised, UAE-attested power of attorney does the signing; escrow and regulated channels carry the funds; the transfer completes at the registration trustee. The full walkthrough is the Non-Resident Buyer’s Blueprint article.

How is my money protected on an off-plan purchase?
Two mechanisms. First, every payment must go to the project’s DLD-supervised escrow account, released against verified construction milestones rather than a developer’s discretion. Second, Oqood interim registration records the unit in your name until the title deed issues at handover. The Developer Reputation Tracker grades the builders before you commit.

Which property visa do I qualify for?
It follows the threshold: AED 2M in property puts the 10-year Golden Visa in range (a mortgaged property qualifies only while the unpaid balance is at or below 50% of the title deed value); AED 750K opens the 2-year Investor Visa; the Retirement Visa runs 5 years and the Remote Work Visa 1 year. Run your exact profile through the Visa Eligibility Checker before assuming.

What rental yield should I expect as a landlord?
Gross yields across the eight core districts run from 3.8% in Emirates Hills to 7.0% in Business Bay, with Dubai Marina at 6.4% and Downtown at 6.0% — all on the apartment base, ZARIN ROI basis. Service charges then take their share: the same Marina unit that yields 6.4% gross nets closer to 5.5% after roughly AED 16,000 in annual charges. The market data dashboard carries the full table.

Are the guides free, and how is ZARIN paid?
The guides are free — no gate, no drip-feed. The desk is paid only when you transact: the 2% buyer-paid commission on a completed purchase, and nothing before it. That is deliberate: the guides exist so that you arrive at the first meeting already knowing what the process, the costs and the numbers look like.

Yes. Every guide on this page is available as a PDF brochure on request via advisory@zarinrealestate.com — useful for sharing with a family office, a lawyer or a co-buyer. If you want the guides applied to a specific brief rather than read in the abstract, book a consultation instead.
