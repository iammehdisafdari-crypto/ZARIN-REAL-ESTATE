Dubai Area Guide · 2026 Edition

Jumeirah Bay
Island

Jumeirah Bay Island is Dubai's seahorse-shaped private island — 128 villa plots completed between 2013 and 2017 under a Nakheel masterplan, its form drawn by Meraas, with a single 300-metre bridge as the only road connection. By transaction value it stands among the most expensive addresses in Dubai, alongside Palm Jumeirah's signature fronds. Built villas average AED 4,200 per sqft in 2026; land alone on sea-facing plots trades at AED 4,000-6,000 per sqft. Entry sits at AED 35M for older interior builds, with signature sea-facing estates at AED 80M-150M+ and the Bulgari Resort & Residences anchoring the island's crown. Roughly 130 villas exist; two to four resales above AED 50M change hands in a typical year. ZARIN's founder has invested in Dubai real estate since 2005 and formally established ZARIN in 2023; this guide profiles the island with the discretion the address requires.

Area Overview

A Private Island, Drawn as a Seahorse

The master development. Nakheel reclaimed Jumeirah Bay Island from the Gulf with the island's seahorse form designed by Meraas; plots were released to private buyers from 2013 and the first villas completed by 2017. The masterplan is austere by design: 128 plots of 12,000-20,000 sqft, a single 300-metre bridge from Jumeirah Beach Road as the only road access, and nothing else — no towers, no retail strip, no through traffic. The Bulgari Resort & Residences, opened at the island's crown, is the sole branded component: its villas, mansion, and hotel occupy the head of the seahorse while every other home on the island is a one-off private build. Reclamation and shaping ran as a single continuous programme, so every plot line, height line, and setback was fixed before the bridge opened — the island reads as one architectural act rather than an accumulation of phases. The result is the closest thing Dubai has to a family-owned island, fifteen minutes from the airport.

Who owns here. The island's ~130 households are, predominantly, estate buyers and family offices holding for generations rather than cycles — owners who chose the address precisely because nothing about it can be replicated or densified. Villas are staff-intensive private estates: architects of record include most of the names that define contemporary Gulf residential architecture. The rhythm of the island is deliberately quiet — walls, mature landscaping, and the sea on three sides — with the Bulgari resort's beach club and restaurants as the single social node. Households run on permanent staff — drivers, chefs, security liaisons — and the island's rhythm is set by school runs past the checkpoint and quiet weekday afternoons; weekends bring family gatherings and the beach club rather than traffic. Turnover is generational: when a villa sells here it is usually an estate settlement, not a trade. Adjacency does the rest: Jumeirah's low-rise beach strip, La Mer, and the schools of Jumeirah and Umm Suqeim sit minutes past the bridge.

The wealth thesis. Jumeirah Bay is a land market wearing villas. On sea-facing plots, land value alone runs AED 4,000-6,000 per sqft — the dirt beneath a signature estate routinely accounts for more than half of the transaction. Five-year price CAGR of 8.5% has compounded with unusually low volatility, because sellers here are never forced: with two to four transactions above AED 50M in a typical year, each trade is a private negotiation between patient parties. A 4.2% gross yield — and realistically 2.8-3% net of the island's AED 30/sqft service charges — is secondary to the preservation argument. The stock is fixed forever; only generational turnover produces listings.

Lifestyle & Amenities

What Living Here Is Actually Like

Feature 01

Absolute Privacy

Privacy here is structural, not procedural: one bridge, one checkpoint, no through traffic, no public beach, no retail on the residential frustum — nothing that generates a stranger. Roughly 130 households share the entire island. For families and public figures, that chokepoint design is what makes Jumeirah Bay the standard against which Dubai discretion is measured. Viewings are by owner consent; there are no open houses, ever.

One bridge · One checkpoint
Feature 02

Bulgari Resort Access

The Bulgari Resort at the island's crown gives residents a five-star social anchor without leaving the security perimeter: the private beach club, Il Ristorante and the marina-front dining, the spa, and the yacht berths. Island residents hold priority access; Bulgari-branded villa owners receive full hotel servicing. Conversely, hotel guests never reach the residential frustum — the benefits flow one way, toward the residents.

Beach club · Spa · Marina berths
Feature 03

Private Beach

Every sea-facing villa holds its own beach frontage — calm, shallow Gulf water on a shoreline shared with no one. The island's outer ring faces open water toward the northwest and the Palm's skyline; sunsets here are the quiet rival of any beach club in the city. Because the island is low-rise by design, no villa ever looks into another's beach — a small sentence that prices in the millions.

Private frontage · Low-rise forever
Feature 04

Single-Bridge Security

The 300-metre bridge is the island's entire road network, screened by a 24/7 checkpoint with vehicle recognition and visitor pre-clearance. Deliveries, contractors, and staff are registered in advance; there is no casual access of any kind. The security operation is funded by the community charge and reports to the island's association. It is the difference between guarded privacy and engineered privacy — and it never sleeps.

24/7 checkpoint · Pre-clearance

Getting Around

The island's single 300-metre bridge is its entire road network: one way on, one way off, onto Jumeirah Beach Road and — four minutes later — Sheikh Zayed Road. From there, Downtown is about 10 minutes, DIFC 12, and DXB around 15 in typical traffic; Dubai Marina runs 25. There is no metro on the island, by design — the nearest Red Line stations sit 8-10 minutes away by car, and most households run chauffeured vehicles through the bridge checkpoint. School runs to the Jumeirah and Umm Suqeim campuses take 10-15 minutes, and the adjacent Jumeirah beach strip covers daily retail without crossing a highway.

Investment Analysis

Yield Is Secondary. That Is the Point.

Jumeirah Bay Island is not an income asset, and no one buys it as one. The market behaves like a private land bank with villas attached: a fixed stock of 128 plots on an island that can never be extended, priced by the square foot of land, and traded — at the top end — a handful of times a year.

Yield & the Preservation Thesis

At a 4.2% gross yield — realistically 2.8-3% net of the island's AED 30/sqft service charges — the income case is secondary by design. The thesis is preservation and optionality: land value alone on sea-facing plots runs AED 4,000-6,000 per sqft, meaning the ground beneath a signature estate frequently accounts for more than half of the transaction. Five-year price CAGR of 8.5% has compounded with remarkably low volatility, because sellers here are never forced: with two to four transactions above AED 50M in a typical year, each trade is a private negotiation between patient parties.

Market History 2020–2026

In 2020, island land traded near AED 2,200-2,500 per sqft of plot. The 2021-2023 trophy-villa boom — Dubai's sharpest re-rating at the top of the market — roughly doubled sea-facing land values, with the Bulgari Mansion setting successive island records and signature plots clearing AED 5,000/sqft by 2023. 2024-2026 brought consolidation rather than correction: transactions thinned, asking prices held, and the top of the market settled at AED 4,000-6,000 per sqft of plot. Built villas now average AED 4,200 per sqft of built-up area — among the highest of any Dubai address.

Outlook 2026–2028

The supply side of the equation is fixed forever: no new plots can be created on the island, and only generational turnover produces listings. ZARIN's base case is 6-9% annual land-value growth through 2028, with the interior ring lagging and sea-facing frontage leading. The genuine risks are liquidity — a well-priced signature estate still needs 6-18 months to find its buyer — and the global wealth cycle, which drives top-of-market demand. For estate buyers and family offices, the practical conclusion: buy the plot, not the building. The land is the asset; the building is the improvement.

Jumeirah Bay Island FAQ

Eight Questions, Answered Properly

The questions ZARIN advisors hear most often about Jumeirah Bay Island. If yours isn't covered, message us on WhatsApp for a direct answer — typically within an hour during business hours.

What is the entry price for Jumeirah Bay Island?
Practical entry sits at AED 35M for an older built villa on an interior plot — effectively a land purchase with a building attached. Contemporary custom builds on interior plots start nearer AED 38-40M; Bulgari Villas open from AED 45M; and signature sea-facing estates trade at AED 80M-150M+, with the land alone on those plots valued at AED 4,000-6,000 per sqft. Roughly 130 villas exist on the island and only a handful trade each year. ZARIN's Private Office maintains the island's off-market register — most opportunities here never reach the portals.

Who can buy on Jumeirah Bay Island?
Anyone. The island is fully freehold: foreign nationals of any nationality can buy, sell, mortgage, and inherit in their own name, with Dubai Land Department title deeds. No residency visa is required, though any purchase above AED 2M qualifies the buyer for the 10-year Golden Visa — at this island's price points, every transaction qualifies. Financing exists but is the exception; most acquisitions are cash, often through family-office structures. ZARIN's legal team handles the full process — KYC, NOC, DLD transfer — under NDA where the buyer prefers.

Do residents get access to the Bulgari Resort?
Yes, in tiers. All island residents can use the Bulgari Resort's facilities — the private beach club, the marina-front dining, the spa — on a member or priority basis. Owners within the Bulgari-branded residences and villas receive full hotel-servicing privileges: housekeeping, concierge, and property management operated to hotel standard. Conversely, hotel guests cannot access the island's private villa zones — the checkpoint filters everything. The resort is the island's social anchor; the villa districts remain, deliberately, the quietest address in Dubai.

How liquid is the Jumeirah Bay resale market?
Thin — by design, and that is the asset's character rather than a flaw. With roughly 130 villas and two to four transactions above AED 50M in a typical year, a well-priced signature estate still needs 6-18 months to find its buyer. Sellers here are rarely forced, which is why values hold through soft cycles: the 2024-2026 consolidation produced thinner volumes, not lower prices. Buyers should underwrite for a long hold and treat liquidity as the trade-off for privacy and land value. ZARIN runs these sales off-market first, matched against a private register of qualified buyers.

What can I build on a Jumeirah Bay plot?
Plots run 12,000-20,000 sqft with ground-plus-two zoning and design review by the community authority. In practice, owners build 12,000-25,000 sqft of built-up area — contemporary estates by the city's leading residential architects are the current norm — with construction typically running 24-36 months at AED 3,000-4,500 per sqft of build cost for specification finishes. Setbacks, height lines, and external materials are governed by the island's design code. ZARIN advises on plot selection with buildability in mind: frontage, orientation, and the placement of neighbouring buildings matter more here than anywhere else in the city.

How secure and private is the island really?
The security architecture is physical and total: a single 300-metre bridge is the only road access, screened by a 24/7 checkpoint with vehicle recognition and visitor pre-clearance. There is no through traffic, no public beach, and no retail on the residential frustum — nothing that generates a stranger. Approximately 130 households share the island with the Bulgari Resort at its crown. For public figures and families who value discretion, this chokepoint design — rather than guards alone — is what makes Jumeirah Bay the benchmark for privacy in Dubai.

What are the service charges on Jumeirah Bay Island?
Community service charges run AED 30 per sqft per year, covering the island's security operation, the bridge checkpoint, landscaping, private beach and common-area upkeep, and road maintenance. On a 15,000 sqft villa that is roughly AED 450,000 per year — meaningful, and correctly treated as the running cost of a private-island address. Systems within each villa — pools, BMS, staff quarters — are privately maintained. ZARIN reviews the service-charge history and community budget on every transaction; the rate has been stable for three consecutive years.

Jumeirah Bay Island vs Palm Jumeirah signature villas — which should I buy?
They answer different briefs. Jumeirah Bay offers structural privacy — 128 plots, one bridge, no through traffic — with land values of AED 4,000-6,000 per sqft on sea-facing plots. The Palm offers scale, beach lifestyle, and choice: signature frond villas trade in a similar per-sqft band but with far more liquidity and a wider quality spread. If your priority is discretion and generational holding, Jumeirah Bay is the stronger fit; if it is water lifestyle, resale optionality, and brand address, the Palm's signature fronds win. ZARIN runs both markets and will show you comparables from each before you commit either way.
