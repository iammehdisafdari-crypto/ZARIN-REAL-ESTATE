Reference

Payment Plan Types Explained

Dubai developers offer five standard off-plan payment plan structures, each calibrated to a different buyer profile and developer financing strategy. The plan that fits best depends on your cash flow profile, your tolerance for construction-period capital deployment, and whether you intend to finance the handover balance via mortgage or pay cash at delivery.

Most Common

60/40 Plan

60 percent of the property price is paid during construction across milestone installments, with the remaining 40 percent due on key handover. This is the most common Dubai plan structure because it balances the developer's cash flow needs against the buyer's exposure to construction risk, leaving a meaningful balance for delivery.

Example Structure
Booking 10% · Start 10% · 20% complete 10% · 60% complete 20% · 80% complete 10% · Handover 40%
Tier 1 Developer

70/30 Plan

70 percent of the purchase price is collected during construction, with 30 percent held back for handover. This aggressive front-loading is favoured by Tier 1 developers like Emaar and Nakheel because it aligns buyer capital deployment with construction progress, reducing developer financing cost and accelerating project completion.

Example Structure
Booking 20% · Start 20% · 40% complete 15% · 70% complete 15% · Handover 30%
Balanced Risk

50/50 Plan

Half the price is paid during construction and half is held back for handover. This balanced structure is popular with mid-tier developers and is often used for projects where construction risk is perceived as higher, since the buyer retains 50 percent of capital until delivery is verified.

Example Structure
Booking 10% · Start 20% · 50% complete 20% · Handover 50%
Cash-Flow Friendly

Post-Handover 50/50

Half the price is paid during construction and the remaining 50 percent is spread over one to three years after key handover. This structure reduces buyer cash flow pressure because rental income from the handed-over unit can subsidize the remaining balance. Popular with DAMAC and Select Group, typically priced 8-15 percent higher than equivalent 60/40 plans.

Example Structure
Booking 10% · Start 15% · 50% complete 15% · Handover 10% · Post-Handover Y1 25% · Post-Handover Y2 25%
First-Time Investor

1% Monthly Plan

Ten percent is paid at booking, forty percent at construction start, and the remaining fifty percent is paid in equal monthly installments of one percent each over fifty months. This structure is the most buyer-friendly for first-time investors because it converts the purchase into predictable monthly outflows that can be matched to salaried income.

Example Structure
Booking 10% · Start 40% · Monthly 1% over 50 months 50%
Methodology

How the Calculator Works

The ZARIN Off-Plan Payment Calculator runs in four transparent steps. Every calculation is performed locally in your browser via JavaScript, so you can adjust inputs freely without worrying about privacy, latency, or server-side storage. The methodology below mirrors the way our advisory team models payment schedules for live clients.

1

Input Property Details

Begin by entering seven inputs that define your off-plan purchase: property price in AED (range 500,000 to 50,000,000), property type (apartment, villa, townhouse, or penthouse), payment plan type (5 standard options), construction duration in months (12-60), post-handover period if applicable (12-36 months), and the DLD fee and commission toggles. The slider and number input on each numeric field stay synchronized, so you can use whichever is faster.

7 inputs · 5 plan types · 60-month max duration
2

Apply Plan Structure

Behind the calculator sits a template library of five standard Dubai payment plan structures, each encoded as an ordered list of milestones with cumulative percentage allocations. When you select a plan type, the calculator loads the corresponding template and prepares to compute amounts against your property price. The templates mirror the DLD-registered milestone schedules used by Tier 1, mid-market, and post-handover specialist developers across Dubai.

5 templates · cumulative % encoding · DLD-aligned
3

Calculate Schedule

The calculator computes the AED amount for each milestone by multiplying your property price by the milestone percentage, then walks forward from the booking date to project a calendar date for each milestone based on your construction duration slider. Post-handover milestones are appended after the handover date using your post-handover period slider. The output is a complete milestone-by-milestone schedule with cumulative percentages.

Review Results

The results panel surfaces seven summary metrics (down payment, construction-period installments, post-handover installments, total property cost, monthly average payment, last payment date, and full schedule), a cost breakdown listing every additional buyer fee (DLD 4 percent, commission 2 percent + VAT, admin AED 4,000, title deed AED 580), and the full schedule table. Frequently Asked Questions

Payment Calculator FAQ

Eight questions on plan selection, missed payments, DLD fees, mortgages, and post-handover structures — answered by the ZARIN advisory team.

What is the most common payment plan for Dubai off-plan property?

The 60/40 plan is the most common Dubai off-plan payment structure, used by a majority of mid-market developers. Under this plan, buyers pay 60 percent of the property price during construction across milestone installments (typically booking, construction start, 20 percent, 60 percent, and 80 percent completion triggers) and the remaining 40 percent on handover when keys are exchanged. The structure balances developer cash flow needs with buyer risk, since a meaningful portion is deferred until delivery. ZARIN's founder has been investing in Dubai real estate since 2005 and has seen this plan survive multiple market cycles as the industry default.

What happens if I miss an off-plan payment in Dubai?

Missing an off-plan payment triggers a defined sequence under UAE real estate law. The developer typically issues a written notice giving the buyer 30 days to cure the default. If the payment remains unpaid after that window, the developer may terminate the Sale & Purchase Agreement, deduct up to 40 percent of the amounts already paid as compensation, and refund the balance. The exact percentage retained depends on how much of the total price has been paid — under AED 60 percent paid means 40 percent can be retained, while above AED 60 percent paid means only 10 percent can be retained. ZARIN advises clients to maintain a cash buffer equal to two upcoming installments to avoid this scenario.

Can I customize my payment plan with the developer?

Yes, to a limited extent. Developers typically publish a standard payment plan per project (most often a 60/40 or 70/30 structure) but are open to negotiated variations for premium buyers committing to high-value units or buying multiple units at once. Common customizations include shifting the booking deposit from 10 percent to 20 percent in exchange for a 2-4 percent price discount, extending the post-handover tail by 6-12 months, or aligning installments with the buyer's known income events. All customizations must be written into the SPA and registered with DLD. ZARIN negotiates these terms on behalf of clients as part of standard advisory.

What is the DLD fee and when do I pay it?

The DLD (Dubai Land Department) transfer fee is a mandatory 4 percent of the property purchase price, payable to the Dubai Land Department at the time of property registration. For off-plan property, this fee is paid at the Oqood pre-registration stage — typically within 30 to 60 days of signing the SPA — and is in addition to the property price. The fee is paid into the project's escrow account or directly to DLD via the developer's conveyancing team. There are no exemptions or reductions for off-plan buyers; even first-time buyers pay the full 4 percent. ZARIN ensures all clients are aware of this cost before signing.

Is the 4% DLD fee on top of the property price?

Yes, the 4 percent DLD fee is a separate government charge levied on top of the agreed property price. If you buy an off-plan apartment for AED 2,000,000, you will pay AED 80,000 in DLD fees at registration, plus a small admin fee (AED 4,000) and a title deed issuance fee (AED 580). Some developers advertise properties as 'DLD-free' or '4 percent DLD included' as a promotional incentive — in those cases the developer effectively absorbs the 4 percent by discounting the list price. ZARIN's calculator includes the 4 percent DLD fee by default (toggle it off if you are evaluating a DLD-free promotion).

Can I get a mortgage on off-plan property in Dubai?

Yes, but with structural differences from ready-property mortgages. During construction you typically pay installments from your own funds into the project escrow account. A mortgage can then be arranged to fund the handover balance, with UAE banks offering up to 50-80 percent loan-to-value depending on the property value and your residency status. Some developers partner with specific banks to offer in-house financing with streamlined approval. The mortgage registration fee is AED 4,020 plus 0.25 percent of the loan amount. ZARIN coordinates with major UAE lenders including Emirates NBD, Mashreq, and ADCB to secure competitive terms for off-plan clients.

What is a post-handover payment plan and who offers it?

A post-handover payment plan spreads a portion of the purchase price over a defined period (typically 1-3 years) after key handover. The most common structure is 50/50 post-handover, where 50 percent is paid during construction and 50 percent is paid in equal installments across the post-handover period. This reduces buyer cash flow pressure because rental income from the handed-over unit can be used to subsidize the remaining payments. DAMAC, Select Group, and Ellington are among the Dubai developers most associated with post-handover plans. The trade-off is that headline prices are typically 8-15 percent higher than equivalent 60/40 plans, reflecting the developer's cost of carrying buyer debt.
