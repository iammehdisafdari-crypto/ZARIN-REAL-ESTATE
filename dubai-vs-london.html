Dubai · vs · London

Dubai vs London

Dubai wins on entry price, yield, and tax; London wins on liquidity, stock depth, and institutional depth. Prime central London (Mayfair, Knightsbridge) trades at £1,800-2,400 per square foot — four to five times Dubai's prime AED 2,200-2,800 — while Dubai delivers 6-7% gross yields against London's 3-4%, with 0% capital gains tax versus the UK's 24% higher rate and a flat 4% transfer fee against SDLT of up to 15%+ for additional and overseas buyers. A £1M budget buys roughly four times the space in Dubai. London answers with 3.5 million homes of deeply liquid stock, the world's most transparent title system, and four centuries of institutional capital. This page compares both cities honestly — data first, judgement second. ZARIN's founder has invested in Dubai real estate since 2005; the firm was formally established in 2023.

Why This Comparison Matters

London and Dubai are the two markets most often shortlisted by the same buyer. British nationals have ranked among the top five foreign investor groups in Dubai every year since 2020, roughly 150,000 UK nationals now live in the UAE, and London-based premium investors increasingly run parallel portfolios — a Knightsbridge townhouse for the balance sheet, a Palm Jumeirah apartment for the yield. The flow runs both ways: Gulf capital has been a fixture of prime central London since the 1970s, and Emirati and Saudi buyers remain active in Mayfair and Belgravia today. The two cities are connected by more than 100 weekly flights and a three-to-four-hour time difference, share English as the language of business, contracts and law, and anchor opposite ends of the same investment corridor. Anyone comparing them is choosing between two genuinely world-class options — not screening a risky alternative against a safe one.

On the numbers, Dubai wins on entry price, yield, and tax — decisively. Prime central London trades at £1,800-2,400 per square foot (roughly AED 8,200-11,000), four to five times Dubai's prime AED 2,200-2,800 in Downtown Dubai and Palm Jumeirah; citywide averages are ~£550/sqft (AED ~2,500) against Dubai's AED 1,500 (~USD 408). Gross yields run 3-4% in London versus 6-7% in Dubai — up to 7.5% on Business Bay studios. Entry costs diverge sharply: Dubai charges a flat 4% DLD transfer fee plus ~2% agent commission and AED 4,580 admin, while a non-resident additional-home buyer in London faces Stamp Duty Land Tax of up to 12% plus surcharges that push the total past 15%. On exit, the UK taxes gains at 24% (higher rate) and estates at 40% above the £325,000 nil-rate band; Dubai charges 0% on both. London answers with 3.5 million homes of deeply liquid stock against Dubai's ~750,000 units, the world's most transparent land registry, and four centuries of institutional track record — a market growing 1% a year in population terms against Dubai's 4-5% simply plays a different game.

Dubai suits yield-led investors, tax-aware owners, and buyers seeking residency: a portfolio landlord targeting 6-7% gross, a professional using the Golden Visa (AED 2M property threshold, 10-year renewable) to anchor a UAE move, or a family wanting a modern, serviced, new-build base for the winter months. London suits buyers who prize liquidity and permanence above cash flow: holders of long-horizon capital who want the deepest resale market on earth, proximity to European and American time zones, and an asset class that has compounded through two centuries of shocks. Many of ZARIN's most satisfied clients own both — London as the store of value, Dubai as the yield engine. ZARIN's founder has been investing in Dubai real estate since 2005, long before the current cycle, and the comparison data below reflects two decades of watching capital move in both directions along this corridor.

Reading the Segment Table

Same budget, different lives. A £1M (AED 4.6M) budget buys a spacious 2-3BR in Dubai Hills Estate with golf-course frontage, or a high-floor Marina two-bed with sea views — in London, roughly a Zone 2-3 one- or two-bedroom flat. At the prime end the gap widens: AED 8M secures a Palm Jumeirah signature-arc apartment or a full-floor Downtown residence with Burj Khalifa views, while the same money is a starter one-bedroom in Mayfair. London's entry market (£400-650K) is genuinely more central than Dubai's AED 700K-1.2M entry tier, which sits in Business Bay, JVC and JLT — central business districts, but without London's 150-year infrastructure patina. Remember too that most central London apartments are leasehold with finite tenure and ground rent, while Dubai apartments are overwhelmingly freehold — a structural difference in what you actually own. At the trophy tier both cities clear in global money, but the Dubai unit typically yields twice as much.

Why Dubai's Net Advantage Is Larger Than Gross Suggests

Dubai's net advantage is larger than the gross gap suggests. Gross, Dubai leads 6.75% to 3.5%; net, the spread is roughly 5.2% to 1.2-1.8% for a UK taxpayer. Three drivers explain the compounding. First, entry duty: London's 15%+ SDLT for an additional-home or non-resident buyer must be amortised over the hold — on a ten-year hold that alone costs about 1.5% of capital per year, roughly half of London's entire gross yield. Second, income tax: Dubai rental profits are untaxed in the UAE, while UK rents are taxed at your marginal rate after a limited deduction set. Third, service economics: Dubai's service charges (AED 10-30/sqft) are fully itemised and competitive, while central London leaseholds add ground rent and escalating service charges to flats that are already depreciating leasehold assets with finite tenure. The result: on a ten-year hold, a Dubai unit returns roughly two-and-a-half to three times the net cash of its London equivalent — before any capital growth differential.

Tax & Ownership

Tax & Ownership Deep Dive

The four tax heads that decide cross-border property returns: what you pay to get in, what you pay to hold, what you pay to get out, and what your heirs pay when you are gone. Compared side by side, city against city, with the 2024-2026 rule changes baked in.

Tax Head 01

Purchase Taxes & Fees

Dubai charges a 4% DLD transfer fee, ~2% agent commission and AED 4,580 in admin — call it 6% all-in, flat regardless of price. London tiers Stamp Duty Land Tax from 2% to 12%, adds a 3% surcharge for additional homes and a further 2% for non-resident buyers (since April 2021) — a non-resident buying a second £1.5M home pays roughly £158,250, more than 10.5%, before legal fees and surveys. Dubai's duty never scales with price or buyer status.

Tax Head 02

Ongoing Taxes

Dubai levies no annual property tax, no wealth tax and no income tax on rents — owners pay building service charges (typically AED 10-30 per sqft annually) and nothing to government. London adds council tax (usually tenant-paid but owner-borne in voids), escalating leasehold service charges and ground rent, and — where a £500K+ home sits inside a company — the Annual Tax on Enveloped Dwellings, £4,150 to £28,140+ for 2025/26. UK rental profit is income-taxed on top at your marginal rate.

Tax Head 03

Exit Taxes (Capital Gains)

Dubai charges 0% capital gains tax on property disposals — the entire gain, however large, is yours. UK capital gains tax on residential property runs at 18% (basic rate) or 24% (higher/additional rate) since 30 October 2024, and applies to UK property even for non-resident sellers. On a £500K gain, a higher-rate seller hands HMRC £120,000; the Dubai seller pays nothing beyond a nominal NOC and admin.

Tax Head 04

Succession & Inheritance

Dubai charges no inheritance tax, but without a registered DIFC Will (English-law succession, recognised by Dubai Courts, ~AED 7,500-15,000) a non-Muslim estate falls under UAE Personal Status Law's fixed fractional shares. The UK applies 40% inheritance tax above the £325,000 nil-rate band (up to £500,000 with the residence band) to UK-domiciled worldwide estates — including Dubai assets. A UK-domiciled owner of both a London house and a Dubai flat faces IHT on both; a non-UK-domiciled owner's Dubai property passes free of UK tax.

Tax rules change. This comparison reflects published rules as of early 2026; ZARIN's cross-border tax desk verifies each client's position with qualified advisors in both jurisdictions before any purchase — the consultation is free and without obligation.

Lifestyle & Practical

Lifestyle & Practical Considerations

Property is a life decision before it is a spreadsheet decision. Four honest comparisons — climate, schooling, healthcare, and safety and language — where both cities have genuine strengths and neither is objectively "better".

Consideration 01

Climate

Dubai guarantees sunshine: 8+ hours daily, 24-32°C from October to April, and a hot, dry 38-42°C June-September that sends midday life indoors. London offers a genuine four-season climate — mild 5-10°C winters with short days, pleasant 18-23°C summers — at the cost of grey weeks and rain in any month. Winter-sun buyers overwhelmingly prefer Dubai; buyers who wilt in heat, or who want gardens, seasons and cool summer evenings, prefer London. Neither is better — they are opposite products.

Consideration 02

Schooling

Both cities school in English to a world standard. Dubai runs 200+ private schools led by the British curriculum (UK EYFS through A-levels), IB and American tracks, at AED 40,000-100,000+ a year; London offers the deeper bench — free state schools including many outstanding ones, elite independents at £15,000-30,000+, and the world's strongest university cluster (Imperial, UCL, LSE). For a British family, London is the default education choice; Dubai delivers an equivalent English education with smaller classes and a sunnier campus, at a price.

Consideration 03

Healthcare

London gives residents the NHS — free at the point of use, with world-class teaching hospitals (Guy's, St Bartholomew's, Great Ormond Street) — though non-residents pay and waiting lists are real. Dubai runs a fully private, insurance-based system: mandatory cover (from ~AED 5,000-10,000 a year for basic plans) buys fast access to excellent hospitals such as the American Hospital Dubai and Rashid. Britons in Dubai describe healthcare as private, fast and out-of-pocket-adjacent; London's is free, excellent and occasionally slow. Both are safe pairs of hands.

Consideration 04

Safety & Language

Dubai ranks among the world's safest cities — violent crime is negligible and the Numbeo safety index sits in the high 80s; petty theft exists but is uncommon. London is safe by megacity standards — far safer than most American peers — but carries higher property crime and late-night hotspots to manage. Language is a non-issue in either: English is the working language of Dubai business, government and schooling, and London's native tongue. A British buyer faces zero language friction in both; the choice is purely lifestyle and economics.

The Decision

Which City Is Right for You?

Five reasons each way, with no sales agenda — roughly a third of ZARIN's advisory conversations end with "buy in your home market" or "buy both", and that is the correct answer for many buyers.

Choose Dubai if…

  • Yield is the priority — 6-7% gross (up to 7.5% Business Bay studios) against London's 3-4% — and roughly 5.2% versus 1.2-1.8% net for a UK taxpayer.
  • Tax efficiency matters — 0% capital gains, 0% annual property tax and 0% inheritance tax in the UAE, against UK CGT at 24%, SDLT at up to 15%+ and IHT at 40%.
  • You want residency — the Golden Visa grants a 10-year renewable visa at the AED 2M property threshold; the UK closed its investor route in 2022.
  • You prefer new-build stock — the majority of Dubai's ~750K units were delivered this century, with pools, gyms and concierge standard rather than premium.
  • You want USD-adjacent stability — the dirham is pegged to the US dollar at 3.6725, removing the sterling volatility that marks the GBP/AED corridor.

Choose London if…

  • Liquidity is the priority — 3.5 million homes and the deepest resale and rental markets in Europe, with 90-day exits realistic at most price points.
  • You value institutional depth — the world's most transparent land registry, 500+ years of title case law, and the deepest professional ecosystem in global property.
  • You want a long-proven store of value — prime London has compounded through two world wars and every modern cycle, and remains the default global safe asset.
  • Your life is time-zoned to Europe and the Americas — London's working day overlaps both, and the city is the world's flight-and-capital crossroads.
  • You need a family education-and-healthcare base — free world-class state schooling, the NHS, and the strongest university cluster in Europe.

Is Dubai really cheaper than London?
Yes — substantially, on every per-square-foot measure. Prime central London (Mayfair, Knightsbridge, Belgravia) trades at £1,800-2,400 per square foot (AED 8,200-11,000) against AED 2,200-2,800 in Downtown Dubai and Palm Jumeirah — a 4-5× gap. Citywide, London averages ~£550/sqft (AED ~2,500) against Dubai's AED 1,500 (~USD 408). In budget terms, £1M buys roughly a Zone 2 one- to two-bedroom flat in London, or a 2-3BR with premium amenities in Dubai Hills Estate or Dubai Marina. The caveat cuts both ways: London's outer-borough stock can match Dubai entry pricing, and prime London is a genuinely different asset class with four centuries of provenance — but on space, specification and newness per pound spent, Dubai wins decisively.
Can I buy property in both Dubai and London?
Yes — there are no exclusivity or residency requirements in either direction, and many ZARIN clients hold both. The UK imposes no restrictions on foreign or expatriate ownership of UK property, and Dubai's freehold zones (covering virtually all investment-grade areas since the 2002 Freehold Decree) are open to all nationalities, including British. The two purchases are legally independent: separate title systems, separate financing, separate tax exposure. The practical considerations are financing (UK expat BTL lenders and UAE banks assess affordability independently), tax residency (which determines where worldwide income and gains are taxed), and succession — a harmonised DIFC Will and UK Will should be drafted together so neither estate is caught by the other's rules. ZARIN's cross-border desk coordinates UK solicitors, UAE counsel and tax advisors in a single workflow.
What tax does a London resident pay on a Dubai property?
UK tax residency, not location, drives the answer. A UK-resident owner pays UK income tax on Dubai rental profits at their marginal rate — the UAE-UK Double Taxation Agreement credits UAE tax (currently zero) but does not exempt UK tax — and UK capital gains tax at 18% or 24% (higher rate, post-October 2024) on disposal, because UK residents are taxed on worldwide gains. New UK arrivals may qualify for the FIG regime (from April 2025), which exempts foreign income and gains — including Dubai rents and disposals — for up to four years for those non-resident for the prior ten. UK inheritance tax at 40% above the nil-rate bands applies to UK-domiciled worldwide estates, Dubai property included. Non-UK-resident owners face no UK tax on Dubai assets at all — the property is simply a UAE asset in a 0% UAE property tax regime.
Which city has better rental yields — Dubai or London?
Dubai, by roughly two-to-one gross and three-to-one net. Dubai averages 6-7% gross citywide (7.5% on Business Bay studios) against London's 3-4% — often 2-3% in prime central postcodes. The net gap is wider: Dubai's only recurring costs are service charges (AED 10-30/sqft) and management, with no property or income tax in the UAE, netting roughly 5.2% on a typical AED 2M apartment. A London equivalent grosses ~3.5% but nets ~1.2-1.8% for a UK taxpayer after service charges, letting fees, maintenance and income tax — before amortising the 15%+ SDLT an additional-home or non-resident buyer pays at entry. London's honest counter-argument is capital preservation and rental resilience from a 3.5M-home market, not yield.
Does buying property give me residency in Dubai or London?
Dubai: yes. Property of AED 2M+ qualifies for the 10-year renewable Golden Visa, with AED 750K+ qualifying for a 2-year investor visa and retirement routes from AED 2M for over-55s. London: no. The UK has no property-led residency route — the Tier 1 (Investor) visa was closed to new applicants in February 2022, and owning UK property confers no immigration status whatsoever. This asymmetry is one of the most under-appreciated differences between the two markets: a Dubai purchase can anchor an entire relocation — schooling, banking, healthcare, business setup — while a London purchase is purely a financial asset. For buyers planning a future UAE move, the Golden Visa is often worth more than the yield spread alone.
Can I get a mortgage in Dubai as a non-resident — and in London as a Dubai resident?
Both are possible, but the terms differ. Dubai non-resident mortgages typically run 50-60% LTV over up to 25 years at roughly 4-5% (2026 rates), with income verification from any country and no UAE credit history required; UAE residents can access up to 80% LTV. London is the mirror image: a Dubai-based British buyer uses the expat buy-to-let market, where specialist lenders offer 70-75% LTV at 1-2% above high-street rates, with verified AED income acceptable; own-use expat residential mortgages exist but are narrower. The practical differences are tax treatment — UK BTL interest relief is restricted to a 20% credit while Dubai has no income tax to deduct against — and currency, since a sterling mortgage against a dirham asset adds a dimension worth modelling first.
What is the currency risk between AED and GBP?
The dirham is pegged to the US dollar at 3.6725 and has been since 1997, so AED risk is really USD risk. Sterling floats freely: GBP/AED has ranged roughly 4.2-4.9 over the past decade, including a sharp GBP fall after the 2022 mini-Budget that briefly made Dubai property around 10% cheaper in sterling terms. For a GBP-earning buyer, a Dubai purchase adds currency exposure in both directions — GBP weakness gains on the asset, a GBP recovery raises the sterling cost of servicing AED-linked costs. Standard mitigations apply: buy at favourable exchange windows and use forward contracts for staged off-plan payments. Remember the mirror image: London property carries the identical FX question for a Dubai-based owner. ZARIN's multi-currency desk prices every quotation in both currencies.
