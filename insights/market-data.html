The Short Version

The Dubai market in one dashboard: a citywide average of AED 1,650/sqft, up 12% year on year, across 38,247 DLD-registered transactions in Q3 2026 and an average gross yield of 6.5%. Business Bay leads the yield table at 7.0% on AED 1,750/sqft; Palm Jumeirah led the quarter’s appreciation; Jumeirah Bay Island anchors the price table at AED 4,200/sqft for 4.2%. Service charges run from roughly AED 12/sqft/yr in Dubai Hills to roughly AED 22 in Downtown — the line between a 6.4% gross and a 5.5% net in Dubai Marina. What Moved in Q3

Two Flows, One Market

Q3 2026’s 38,247 transactions split along a line the desk watches closely: the yield-seeking flow and the appreciation-seeking flow. The yield-seekers concentrated where income is thickest — Business Bay, at 7.0% gross on AED 1,750/sqft, and Dubai Marina, at 6.4% on AED 1,850 — districts where the entry ticket is smaller and the rent carries more of the return.

The appreciation-seekers ran to scarcity: Palm Jumeirah, the quarter’s top appreciation area at AED 2,800/sqft and 4.9% gross, and Jumeirah Bay Island at AED 4,200/sqft and 4.2%, where the return arrives as capital rather than rent. Blended, the city closed the quarter at AED 1,650/sqft, up 12% year on year.

Service Charge Index

The Bill After the Purchase

Service charges are the recurring cost of owning in a managed district, and the spread between districts is wide enough to move a yield decision. The figures below follow the RERA service-charge index, read at district and building-class level; individual buildings vary around their district benchmark.

What the Charge Includes

Security

24-hour staffing, access control and monitoring of the building or community perimeter.

Landscaping

Communal grounds, podium gardens and streetscape upkeep within the community boundary.

Common-Area Maintenance

Corridors, lifts, gyms, pools, plant rooms and the shared fabric of the building.

Community Management

The management layer that administers the budget, contracts and owners’ association obligations.

Run the district figure against a specific unit with the service-charge calculator, or see the same index inside the quarterly editions on the market reports page.

Rental Yield Tracking

Gross Is the Headline. Net Is the Paycheque.

Gross yield is annual rent divided by price — the number in the eight-area table, and the only number most commentary quotes. Net yield starts subtracting: service charges first, as the largest and most predictable deduction, then management fees, maintenance and voids where they apply.

The spread between the two is not a rounding error. In Dubai Marina, the district’s 6.4% gross becomes closer to 5.5% net once the annual service-charge bill is paid — on the worked example opposite, nearly a full percentage point of yield, every year, for as long as you own the unit.

This is why the desk quotes both, and why the yield table pairs with the service-charge index above rather than standing alone. The ROI calculator runs the same arithmetic on any unit you are considering in Dubai Marina or elsewhere.

Data Sources & Methodology

Where Every Number Comes From

A number you cannot source is an opinion, and opinions are marked as such. The discipline is not decoration — the founder has demanded a source for every figure he has traded on since 2005, and this page is held to the same standard by the firm established in 2023 to publish it.

DLD Transaction Records

Prices and volumes come from registered transfers at the Dubai Land Department — what actually cleared, not what a portal asked.

RERA Service-Charge Index

Ownership costs follow the RERA index, read at district and building-class level, with district benchmarks shown as approximations.

Refreshed Every 24 Hours

The price, volume and yield series refresh on a 24-hour cycle from the transaction record — the page you read tonight was built from yesterday’s registry.

ZARIN ROI Basis (2026)

Yields are achieved rents over price, quoted gross, on a consistent apartment base — the same basis the advisory desk uses with paying clients.

When a basis changes, the change is logged rather than silently absorbed: methodology changes affecting the analysis are recorded in the investment guide changelog, and restatements — DLD revises late registrations, as registries do — are noted on the page they affect.

Limitations & Honest Caveats

What This Page Cannot Tell You

A dashboard earns trust by stating its limits as plainly as its headlines. Three caveats belong alongside every chart above.

01

Averages Blend Stock

A citywide AED 1,650/sqft mixes a AED 4,200 Jumeirah Bay Island trophy with entry stock across the wider market. The district-level table is the honest unit of comparison; the citywide figure is context, never a quote.

02

Past Performance Is Not a Promise

+12% year on year describes the twelve months to Q3 2026 — not the next four quarters. The data is a record of what happened, and the record is the only thing a honest desk will guarantee.

03

Indicative, Not a Valuation

This dashboard is indicative market data, not a valuation of any property. No figure here should price a deal, support a loan or settle an estate.

Market Data FAQ

Eight Questions About the Numbers

Sources, bases, villa premiums and the gross-versus-net gap — the questions readers ask before they trust a dashboard. If yours is not here, write to the desk.

How current is the data on this page?
The price, volume and yield series are refreshed every 24 hours from Dubai Land Department transaction records; the service-charge figures follow the RERA service-charge index at its own publication cadence. Each block on the page carries its source line, and the quarterly market reports freeze the same series into a citable edition.

What is the ZARIN ROI basis?
The house method for yields: achieved rents divided by purchase price, quoted gross, on a consistent apartment base — the 2026 vintage of the basis. Reconciling to achieved rather than asking rents costs the table a few tenths of a percentage point and buys it honesty. Where an estimate intrudes, it is labelled as one, per the methodology above.

Why does Business Bay yield more than Palm Jumeirah?
Price, mostly. Business Bay trades at AED 1,750/sqft against Palm Jumeirah’s AED 2,800, while both districts rent strongly — so the same rent buys more yield per dirham in Business Bay: 7.0% gross against 4.9%. The compensation runs the other way in capital: Q3 2026 put Palm Jumeirah top of the appreciation table. Yield buys income; price buys growth.

Is this dashboard a valuation of my property?
No. This page is indicative market data — useful for context, wrong for a decision letter. For a property-specific number, use the free Market Price Assessment: an advisory indicative appraisal by the ZARIN desk that does not replace a bank or DLD valuation.

What do service charges actually cover?
Four buckets: security (staffing and access control), landscaping of the communal grounds, common-area maintenance (corridors, lifts, gyms, pools, plant) and community management of the budget and contracts. District benchmarks run from roughly AED 12/sqft/yr in Dubai Hills to roughly AED 22 in Downtown on the RERA index; individual buildings vary around their district figure.

How do villa prices compare to the apartment base?
Villa-dominant areas trade roughly 10–15% above the apartment base shown, which is why the table quotes Emirates Hills on its villa base of AED 1,900/sqft rather than an apartment figure. The premium is not noise — it is the price of land, privacy and scarcity, and it is shown separately so the apartment districts compare cleanly.

Why is net yield lower than gross?
Because service charges are paid before you are. The Dubai Marina worked example above makes it concrete: a 1,000 sqft unit at AED 1,850/sqft buys at AED 1.85M, rents at roughly AED 118,400 gross (6.4%), and nets AED 102,400 after AED 16,000 in service charges — close to 5.5%. Management fees, maintenance and voids, where they apply, come on top of that.

Where can I get this data in report form?
In the quarterly market reports: the Q3 2026 edition runs 42 pages and was published in October 2026, with the full library on the market reports page. If you want to run the numbers on your own brief instead, the calculators take the same series as inputs.

The frozen, citable versions of these series live in the quarterly market reports.
