Heritage · Author Profiles

Who Writes
What You Read

A Dubai property decision rests on numbers, and numbers rest on people. This page names the desks that write ZARIN’s 200+ articles, the advisors who review them, and the standards — sources, AI use, corrections — that govern every published figure.

The Short Version

ZARIN’s content is written by three in-house desks — ZARIN Editorial (founder-led buyer guidance), ZARIN Research (market data and DLD/RERA analysis) and the ZARIN Visa Team (residency pathways) — and every page is reviewed and signed off by a named senior advisor before publication. The firm was founded in 2023 by an investor active in Dubai since 2005, and the content inherits that bias: data first, DLD and RERA as primary sources, no paid placements, and no developer paying for coverage. We use AI-assisted research and drafting tooling, disclosed in full on our AI disclosure page, and nothing is published unread. If you think a number is wrong, challenge it by email — corrections are made visibly and logged, not quietly. Accountability is the product on this website as much as advice is.

Accountability, In Writing

Why Bylines Matter

A property article makes claims about your money: what an area trades at, what a visa requires, what a developer has actually delivered. Search engines grade those claims on experience, expertise, authority and trust — E-E-A-T — but the older test is simpler: would the author say it to your face, with their name attached? Every ZARIN article passes that test before it passes any other.

That is why every article on our insights hub carries a named desk — ZARIN Editorial, ZARIN Research or the ZARIN Visa Team — and a named reviewing advisor. The desk tells you who produced the work; the advisor tells you who answer for it. Anonymity is how bad numbers survive; bylines are how they die.

The convention has one deliberate exception: the founder writes without a personal name, in line with how the whole firm presents him. His desk byline — ZARIN Editorial — and his letter carry the accountability instead.

A Byline Guarantees
  • A named desk produced it — in-house, not syndicated, not farmed.
  • A named senior advisor reviewed it and can defend every figure.
  • A date and data basis are stated — no evergreen numbers posing as news.
  • A corrections route that reaches a person, not a ticketing system.

The House Rules

Editorial Standards

Four rules govern everything we publish, from a 200-word glossary entry to the 86-page annual report. They exist because our readers use this content to move money — and because the founder, who has had his own capital at risk since 2005, refuses to publish a number he would not trade on.

The standards apply to the calculators too: the seven free tools on the calculators page run on the same canonical data as the articles, which is why their outputs match our advisors’ spreadsheets.

01

Data first, adjectives last

Every claim that can carry a number does. Where we state a yield, a fee or a threshold, the figure is sourced, dated and reproducible — and where it is an estimate, the article says so in the same sentence.

02

DLD and RERA are primary sources

Recorded transactions beat asking prices; regulator rules beat forum rumour. Transaction data comes from the Dubai Land Department, tenancy and escrow rules from RERA, visa terms from official programme rules.

03

No paid placements, anywhere

Developers do not pay for coverage. Listings are not ranked by commercial relationship. Our income is the 2% buyer-paid commission on completed purchases — so the content serves the reader, or it does not publish.

04

Dates and data bases on every page

An undated number is an unaccountable number. Articles carry their data basis — quarter, sample, source — and are updated when the underlying data moves, not when it is convenient.

Disclosed, Not Hidden

How We Use AI

We use AI-assisted research and drafting tooling in our content workflow, and we say so plainly rather than in a footer written for lawyers. The full disclosure — what the tooling does, what it never does, and who remains accountable — lives on our AI disclosure page.

The boundary is simple: AI assists; people answer. Machine output is a first draft of prose and a research aid — never a source of numbers, never a sign-off, never a substitute for the advisor whose name carries the page. Every published page is reviewed and corrected by a named senior advisor before it goes live.

We hold this position because our readers make six and seven-figure decisions on this content. A wrong number printed by a machine is still a wrong number — and at ZARIN it would still be a named person’s responsibility.

What the tooling does
  • Drafts structure and prose from outlines written by the desks
  • Surfaces source material for researchers to verify at origin
  • Checks drafts for internal consistency against our canonical data
What the tooling never does
  • Generate market figures — numbers come from DLD, RERA and official sources only
  • Publish anything unread — a named advisor reviews and signs off every page
  • Replace judgement — investment calls remain human, senior and named
Challenge Any Number

Corrections Policy

If you believe a figure on this website is wrong, you are invited to prove it. Email advisory@zarinrealestate.com with the page, the number and your source. The reviewing advisor for that topic answers with the underlying data — the same records we publish from, not a curated screenshot.

When we are wrong, the page is corrected and the correction is logged: what changed, why, and when. Silent edits are how trust dies quietly; we prefer the louder version. When the regulator revises data, affected pages are updated on the same basis — and our quarterly market reports restate the revised series in full.

01

You challenge

One email: the page, the figure, your source. No form, no ticket number, no waiting room. Corrections reach the advisor who signed off the page.

02

We verify

The reviewing advisor re-pulls the underlying DLD, RERA or programme data and answers with it — whichever way it settles. You see the same records we do.

03

We correct & log

If the figure is wrong, the page is fixed and the change is logged — what moved, why, and when. If it was right, you get the receipts. Both outcomes are published honestly.

Why we want your challenges

A firm that has been publishing data-led content since it was established in 2023 learns more from one well-sourced challenge than from a hundred pageviews. The testimonials say the same about our advisory: the value is in the pushback.

Editorial FAQ

Eight Questions About Our Content

Who writes, who reviews, how data is sourced, how AI is used and what happens when a number is wrong — answered in full. If your question is not here, ask us directly.

Who writes ZARIN's articles?
Three in-house content desks: ZARIN Editorial (founder-led buyer guidance), ZARIN Research (market data and DLD/RERA analysis) and the ZARIN Visa Team (residency pathways). Every published page is then reviewed and signed off by a named senior advisor from our team before it goes live. No freelancers, no ghostwriters, no content farms.

Why do articles carry desk bylines rather than individual names?
Because a byline should tell you who is accountable, not just who typed. A desk byline names the team that produced the work and the named advisor who reviewed it — and desks at ZARIN map to people, not to job titles. It also keeps the focus on the advice rather than on personalities, the same convention under which the founder writes his letter without a personal byline.

Can I contact the author of an article?
Yes. Write to advisory@zarinrealestate.com, reference the article and the byline, and your email reaches that desk directly — the desks are small enough that nothing is routed through a nameless inbox. Questions about a specific number go to the reviewing advisor who signed off on it. Substantive questions about your own file are better served by a consultation.

Does ZARIN accept sponsored content or paid placements?
No. Developers do not pay for coverage, listings carry no paid ranking, and no article on this website was bought. Our income is the 2% buyer-paid commission on completed purchases — which means our content has exactly one client to serve: the reader deciding whether to buy. When we decline to cover a project, that is editorial, not commercial.

How current is the market data in your articles?
Transactional data refreshes as DLD publishes it, and our area benchmarks are re-stamped each quarter — the Q3 2026 market report (42 pages, October 2026) is the current anchor. Every article states its data basis, and when DLD revises a figure, the article is updated rather than left quietly wrong. The live view sits on our market data page.

Does ZARIN use AI to write its content?
We use AI-assisted research and drafting tooling, disclosed in full on our AI disclosure page. What we do not do is publish machine output unread: every page is reviewed, corrected and signed off by a named senior advisor who owns its accuracy. The numbers come from DLD and RERA sources; the judgement comes from people who have transacted since 2005.

What sources does ZARIN use for market data?
Primary regulatory sources first: Dubai Land Department transaction records, RERA indices and escrow rules, Ejari-derived rental evidence, and official visa programme terms. Secondary market colour is labelled as such and never presented as recorded data. Where an estimate is ours — a yield band, a premium range — the article says so. The full methodology sits in our quarterly reports.

I think a number is wrong — how do I get it corrected?
Challenge it. Email advisory@zarinrealestate.com with the page, the figure and your source, and the reviewing advisor for that topic will answer with the underlying data. If we are wrong, the page is corrected and the correction is logged — visibly, not silently. If we are right, you will see exactly why. Either outcome makes our insights library more useful.
