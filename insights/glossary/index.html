The Short Version

104 definitions, one page, no sales fog. The essentials in a paragraph: buying costs in Dubai are the DLD transfer fee of 4%, ZARIN’s buyer-paid commission of 2% and AED 4,580 in admin — AED 124,580 all-in on an AED 2M cash purchase (6.2%), or AED 132,830 financed at 60% LTV (6.6%). If a definition ever disagrees with a DLD or RERA publication, the publication wins; tell us and it will be corrected in the next quarterly review.

Authorities & Regulation

The institutions whose signatures make a Dubai property real: who registers, who regulates, who arbitrates — and the apps that let you check all of it yourself, in minutes.

Central Bank of the UAE
The federal regulator that sets the mortgage rules every Dubai bank must follow: loan-to-value caps, the 50% ceiling on debt burden ratios and the caps on early-settlement fees. It regulates the lenders; the DLD governs the land.
DEWA (Dubai Electricity and Water Authority)
The emirate’s electricity and water utility. Moving the account into your name at handover, against a refundable deposit, is one of the first after-sales tasks; district cooling in many towers is billed separately.
DET (Department of Economy and Tourism)
The government department that issues business licences — including the holiday-home permits that let a residential unit be let on short stays. Where the DLD regulates property, DET regulates the business of letting it.
Dubai 2040 Urban Master Plan
The government’s land-use blueprint for how the city grows to 2040 — where density, public beaches, green corridors and new population centres are planned. Long-horizon investors read it because infrastructure follows the plan, and value follows the infrastructure.
Dubai Brokers app
The DLD app that verifies, in seconds, whether the person presenting a listing holds a valid brokerage licence. ZARIN appears there — as a RERA-licensed brokerage under the DLD — and so should anyone you transact with. Check before you view.
Dubai Courts
The emirate’s civil and criminal courts — the final venue for property disputes that fall outside the specialist tribunals. Most routine real-estate disagreements never reach them: tenancy matters go to the Rental Dispute Centre, and market conduct is regulated by RERA.
Dubai Land Department (DLD)
The government body that records every property transaction, issues title deeds and regulates the market — the source of the daily transaction data behind this site’s market data dashboard. When a transfer completes in Dubai, it completes at the DLD.
Dubai REST app
The DLD’s smart-services app: title deed verification, transaction tracking and government services without office visits. Buyers use it to confirm a deed’s details in minutes — including the mortgage recorded against it.
DLD Property Management
The DLD division supervising jointly owned property: service-charge audits through the Mollak system, administration of owners’ committees and oversight of the management companies that run Dubai’s towers.
Escrow Account Regime (Law No. 8 of 2007)
The law that requires every off-plan buyer’s money to be paid into a project-specific escrow account supervised by the DLD, released only against verified construction milestones. It is the single most important protection in off-plan buying — the off-plan investment guide covers how it works in practice.
GDRFA (General Directorate of Residency and Foreigners Affairs)
The authority that processes residence visas, entry permits and Emirates ID registration. Property-backed filings run through DLD channels first — the title deed is the evidence — and the visa itself is issued by the GDRFA.
Notary Public
The office that gives documents the notarisation Dubai’s system requires to carry legal weight — powers of attorney above all. A POA executed abroad must be notarised in the issuing country and attested for the UAE before it works here.
Real Estate Brokerage Licence
The RERA licence a firm must hold — and renew annually, with continuing training — to legally market and transact Dubai property. ZARIN operates as a RERA-licensed brokerage under the DLD; the licence is verifiable through the Dubai Brokers app.
Registration Trustee Office
The DLD-authorised offices where property transfers physically happen: identities verified, balances settled, and the new title deed issued the same day. Your transfer appointment is booked at a trustee office — not at a DLD headquarters counter.
Rental Dispute Centre (RDC)
The specialist tribunal for tenancy disputes — deposits, renewals, evictions, maintenance — with filing fees that scale with the annual rent. It exists so a landlord-tenant disagreement does not need a full court case, and most claims settle well before hearing.
RERA (Real Estate Regulatory Agency)
The DLD’s regulatory arm: it licenses brokers and developers, polices advertising through Trakheesi, supervises escrow accounts, and publishes the rental index and service-charge index that anchor most of the numbers on this site.
RERA Rental Index
RERA’s benchmark of market rents by area and unit type. It matters most at renewal: Decree 43 caps any increase according to how far the current rent sits below this index.
Trakheesi
The DLD permit system for marketing property: every listing, print advert or billboard must carry a Trakheesi permit number. An advert without one is marketing something that may not legally be for sale.

Transaction & Documents

The paperwork path from agreed offer to issued title deed — every document a Dubai purchase touches, roughly in the order you will meet it.

Assignment (Off-Plan Resale)
Selling an off-plan unit before completion by transferring the Oqood-registered contract to a new buyer with the developer’s NOC — typically against an NOC fee of AED 500–5,000. The premium or discount to your original price is yours to negotiate; the Market Price Assessment prices the position before you list it.
Built-up Area (BUA)
Everything under roof that counts as sellable space — the unit plus its balcony share, and for a villa the house footprint. Dubai quotes prices per square foot of BUA; the citywide average in Q3 2026 was AED 1,650.
Completion Certificate
The official confirmation that a building is finished to plan and fit for occupation. On an off-plan purchase it is the document that unlocks title deed issuance and handover — no certificate, no keys, no deed.
Ejari
The DLD’s tenancy registration system. Every Dubai lease must be registered: it is what makes a tenancy enforceable at the Rental Dispute Centre, and what a tenant needs for DEWA, schooling and visa evidence. The after-sales desk files it as part of the letting setup.
Floor Plan
The unit-level drawing showing rooms and dimensions. Compare it against the built-up area on the contract — marketed layout areas sometimes count balcony and podium shares the title deed later itemises differently.
Form F (MOU)
The standard DLD contract through which resales are agreed: price, deposit (usually 10%), completion date and each party’s obligations. Once both parties sign, the sale is bound — there is no chain to withdraw into. The buying advisory page walks the sequence around it.
Handover
The day keys transfer and ownership practically begins: snagging walkthrough, service-charge account opening, DEWA registration and — if letting — Ejari. On an off-plan purchase it follows the Completion Certificate; on a resale, the transfer appointment itself.
Mortgage Clearance (Seller's Loan)
If the seller has a loan, the transfer cannot complete until their bank issues a release letter confirming the mortgage will be cleared with the sale proceeds and the title freed. Coordinating that letter is one of the common causes of delay in the 30-day transfer calendar — and one of the reasons it is managed, not waited on.
NOC (No-Objection Certificate)
The developer’s certificate confirming a property is clear to transfer — no unpaid service charges, no disputes. No transfer appointment happens without it, and on an off-plan assignment the developer’s NOC is equally mandatory.
Oqood Registration (Interim Register)
The DLD’s interim register for off-plan sales: your contract is recorded against the land before the building exists, converting a promise on paper into a registered property right. On an off-plan purchase, the 4% DLD fee is paid at Oqood registration on the contract price.
Plot Area
The land a property sits on — the number that matters for villas and townhouses. Land underpins the villa premium: villa-dominant areas trade 10–15% above the apartment base per square foot, and land scarcity is most of why.
Power of Attorney (POA)
A notarised authorisation for someone else to view, sign, attend the transfer and collect keys on your behalf. Most of ZARIN’s overseas buyers never fly in — a POA executed and attested abroad does the signing.
Reservation Agreement
The document that takes a unit off the market for a short period against a modest booking deposit, ahead of the SPA on off-plan or Form F on a resale. Read the refund terms before signing — they vary by developer and are the whole content of the agreement.
Sales & Purchase Agreement (SPA)
The full contract on an off-plan purchase: payment schedule, completion date, unit specification, and the assignment and delay provisions. Due diligence reads it line by line — the delay clause matters more than anything the brochure promises.
Site Plan
The drawing of the whole project: tower footprints, orientation, amenities and setbacks. On it sit the questions brochures blur — principally how close the neighbouring tower stands, and what that does to your view corridor.
Snagging Report
The itemised defect list from a professional inspection at handover — cosmetic and structural. Developers must remedy within the one-year defect liability window, and ZARIN’s after-sales desk runs the audit while that window is open.
Title Deed
The DLD-issued proof of ownership — the document every other step exists to produce. It records the owner, the plot and any mortgage; buyers verify it through the Dubai REST app in minutes before committing a dirham.
Transfer Day (Trustee Appointment)
The appointment at a Registration Trustee Office where the balance is paid, the old deed cancels and the new one issues. With the NOC done and any mortgage cleared, a straightforward resale closes in about 30 days from Form F — and most of that calendar is those two documents.

Fees & Taxes

What a Dubai transaction actually costs. There is no annual property tax and no capital-gains tax — instead, a fee schedule that stands in for a tax code, quoted here with the canonical numbers.

Agency Commission (2%)
Two percent of the purchase price, buyer-paid, per Dubai market convention — ZARIN’s only fee on a purchase, itemised in writing before you commit. On an AED 2,000,000 property: AED 40,000.
DLD Admin Fee (AED 4,580)
The fixed AED 4,580 covering title deed issuance and registration on a resale — the flat line item that sits behind the percentages and completes the cash-cost arithmetic.
DLD Transfer Fee (4%)
Four percent of the purchase price paid to the Land Department on every transfer — resale or off-plan — and the largest single transaction cost in Dubai. On AED 2,000,000: AED 80,000.
Dubai's Tax Position (No Property or Capital-Gains Tax)
Dubai levies no annual property tax and no capital-gains tax on individuals: owners pay transaction fees — the 4% transfer fee on buying, the 2% commission — and annual service charges, not recurring taxes on ownership or resale profit. Transactions are fee-based, and the fee schedule is effectively the tax code.
Ejari Fee
The flat fee to register a tenancy — AED 220 through the official portal, modestly more via a typing centre. It buys enforceability at the Rental Dispute Centre; registration is not optional.
Escrow Administration
The cost of opening and operating a project’s DLD-supervised escrow account — borne by the developer and reflected in project costing rather than billed to buyers. Its significance is not the size but the discipline: every drawdown requires milestone certification.
Mortgage Registration Fee (0.25% of Loan)
A quarter of one percent of the loan amount, paid to the DLD when a mortgage is registered against the title. On a 60% loan-to-value mortgage against an AED 2,000,000 property: AED 3,000.
NOC Fees (AED 500–5,000)
The developer-set charge — AED 500–5,000 across the market — for the no-objection certificate a transfer or assignment needs. Budget at the top of the band and be pleasantly surprised.
Off-Plan Registration Costs
On an off-plan purchase the 4% DLD fee is collected at Oqood registration on the contract price, together with modest registration admin. The levy is the same as a resale — the timing is earlier, which is exactly why escrow protection exists.
Service Charges (RERA Index)
Annual per-square-foot fees for operating the building, set within the RERA service-charge index: roughly AED 12 per sqft in Dubai Hills Estate, AED 18 in Dubai Marina, AED 20 in Business Bay and AED 22 in Downtown Dubai. A 1,000 sqft Marina unit therefore carries about AED 18,000 a year — the number that separates gross from net yield.
Total Acquisition Cost (6.2% / 6.6%)
Everything a cash buyer pays: 4% + 2% + AED 4,580 = AED 124,580 on an AED 2,000,000 purchase, or 6.2%. Financed at 60% loan-to-value, the mortgage registration fee and bank costs lift the total to about AED 132,830 — 6.6%. The buying advisory page reconstructs both columns from the price alone.
Utility Connection Deposits (DEWA)
Refundable deposits to put electricity and water in your name — about AED 2,000 for an apartment and AED 4,000 for a villa, returned when the account closes. Separate from, and smaller than, most buyers expect.
Valuation Fee
The bank’s charge for valuing the property before lending — typically a few thousand dirhams on a mainstream purchase. The valuation, not the price you agreed, is what caps the loan.
VAT (5%)
Five percent on commercial property transactions and on agency commissions; residential resales are exempt and first supplies of new residential property are zero-rated. In practice a residential buyer’s VAT exposure is the 5% on the brokerage fee.

Ownership Types

The legal shapes Dubai property can take — freehold and its civil-law cousins — and the product types built on top of them.

Branded Residence
A property built and serviced under a luxury hotel or lifestyle brand — with the brand’s management standards, staffing and pricing to match. The current stock shows the premium; underwrite it as liquidity and let, not as a substitute for location arithmetic.
Commercial vs Residential Title
Commercial titles — offices, retail — trade at different yields, attract 5% VAT on sale and follow different mortgage rules. Before the numbers are set on a “mixed-use” unit, establish which title it actually carries.
Fractional Ownership
Co-ownership of a single title in shares, enabled by recent DLD rules, with usage and exit rights set by agreement. It lowers the entry point — and adds co-owner governance to the due-diligence list.
Freehold
Outright, perpetual ownership of the unit and its share of the land, open to buyers of any nationality in designated zones — the structure behind every district this site covers, from Business Bay to Palm Jumeirah. The areas hub lists the freehold map.
Jointly Owned Property (Strata)
The legal framework for apartments: you own your unit outright and share the common areas with co-owners under a jointly owned property declaration, administered through an owners’ committee. Service charges fund the shared parts; Mollak audits them.
Leasehold
A fixed-term right — commonly up to 99 years — to use property whose land belongs to someone else, without owning it. Most resale transactions ZARIN handles are freehold; leasehold appears in specific older districts and commercial ground arrangements.
Musataha
A registrable right to use and develop another party’s land for up to 50 years, renewable. With usufruct, it is one of Dubai’s civil-law property rights — distinct from freehold and leasehold, and occasionally exactly the right instrument.
Off-Plan vs Ready
Off-plan buys before construction completes: payment plans, Oqood, escrow and delivery risk, at keener pricing — the off-plan hub covers the machinery. Ready (secondary) buys an existing deed: roughly 30-day transfers and immediate letting. The trade is terms today against certainty tomorrow.
Serviced Apartment
A unit operated like a hotel suite — housekeeping, front desk, flexible stays — individually titled but often locked into a management programme. Check the management contract’s term and exit before treating it as a normal rental asset.
Usufruct
The registered right to use and take the income from a property you do not own — for up to 99 years — without title transferring. Distinct from Musataha, which adds the right to develop.

Financing

Mortgage mechanics from EIBOR to early settlement: the Central Bank caps, the bank arithmetic and the terms that decide what you can borrow — and what it costs to change your mind later.

Arrangement & Processing Fees
The bank’s charge for setting up the loan — typically 0.5–1% of the amount borrowed, sometimes capped. Negotiable at the margin, and a real line in the 6.6% financed-cost arithmetic.
Bank Valuation vs Purchase Price
The bank lends against its valuation, not the price you agreed. If it values low, the down payment grows in cash terms on transfer day — the single most common surprise for buyers in a fast-moving market.
Buyer's Bank vs Developer's Bank
Off-plan buyers may use any mortgage lender, but a developer’s partner banks pre-approve its projects and sometimes offer construction-period terms. Compare both — the developer’s bank is convenience, not automatically the better rate.
Debt Burden Ratio (DBR)
The share of income the Central Bank allows to go to debt service — capped at 50% including the new mortgage. For most resident buyers it binds long before loan-to-value does.
Early Settlement Fee
The charge for repaying a mortgage early — capped by Central Bank rules at 1% of the outstanding balance or AED 10,000, whichever is lower. Material for anyone planning a resale or refinancing within the first few years.
EIBOR
The Emirates Interbank Offered Rate — the benchmark most Dubai variable-rate mortgages price against, typically EIBOR plus a fixed bank margin. When a Dubai rate is quoted, it is usually a spread over EIBOR.
Fixed vs Variable Rate
Fixed holds for an introductory period — commonly one to five years — then reverts; variable tracks EIBOR plus margin from day one. Fixed buys certainty of payment, not necessarily a cheaper one.
Islamic Mortgage (Ijara / Diminishing Musharaka)
Financing structured to comply with Sharia — Ijara (lease-to-own) or diminishing Musharaka (shared equity bought out over time) — economically similar to a conventional loan. Most Dubai banks offer both windows; compare them on the same sheet.
Loan Tenor
The repayment term — commonly up to 25 years, shortened as the oldest borrower approaches retirement age. Term length moves the monthly payment more than a rate hunt does; model it first in the mortgage calculator.
Loan-to-Value (LTV)
The loan as a share of the property’s value, capped by Central Bank rules: up to 80% for expatriate residents on a first property (higher for UAE nationals, lower above AED 5M and for subsequent properties), and 50–60% for non-residents.
Mortgage Life Insurance (Takaful)
Banks require life cover over the borrower — usually a simple takaful policy costing a few tenths of a percent of the outstanding loan annually, assigned to the bank before the loan funds.
Mortgage Registration
Recording the bank’s charge against the title deed at the DLD — 0.25% of the loan. The bank holds the deed until the loan clears; releasing it at the end is its own small process.
Non-Resident Mortgage (50–60% LTV)
Lending to buyers resident abroad, typically at 50–60% loan-to-value through partner banks, with income evidenced from overseas. ZARIN’s desk coordinates introductions to the banks active in this segment.
Pre-Approval (Agreement in Principle)
The bank’s conditional commitment stating what it will lend against your income before you find the property. It converts you from browser to buyer on the day the right unit appears.
Rate Cap
A contractual ceiling on how far a variable rate may rise over a period. Rare but valuable in a rising-rate cycle — ask specifically, because “capped” and “discounted” are different promises.
Refinancing
Replacing an existing mortgage with a new one — to release equity, shorten the tenor or cut the margin. The front-end costs repeat (valuation, 0.25% registration, arrangement), so run the payback arithmetic before signing.
Rental Offset
The credit banks give verified rental income when calculating affordability — commonly up to 70% of documented rent counting toward serviceability. It is how landlords grow portfolios on the strength of the rent roll.

Rentals & Yields

The rules and arithmetic of Dubai tenancies: landlord rights, tenant notice, rent caps and what separates gross yield from what you actually keep.

Cheque Convention
Dubai rents are quoted annually but paid in post-dated cheques — one, two or four a year. Fewer cheques mean a higher headline rent: the single-cheque premium is the tenant paying for the landlord’s certainty.
Chiller-Free
A lease in which the landlord bears district-cooling charges — in Dubai towers, a difference measured in thousands of dirhams a year. Chiller-free compares only against chiller-paid equivalents; it does not make the rent cheap.
Decree 43 (Rental Cap)
The rule that caps rent increases at renewal: the allowed increase runs from zero — if the rent sits within 10% of the RERA Rental Index benchmark — up to a 20% ceiling, in steps, the further below market the rent sits.
Eviction for Landlord's Own Use
A landlord may end a tenancy to occupy or redevelop — but only on 12 months’ notice, properly served through a notary, and only within the law’s limits. Anything shorter is not an eviction; it is a negotiation.
Furnished vs Unfurnished
Unfurnished is the Dubai default and carries the standard 5% security deposit; furnished units command a premium that rarely survives depreciation honestly accounted. Run both gross yields — the gap narrows once furniture cost and replacement enter.
Gross Yield
Annual rent divided by price — the headline number in Dubai’s market tables. Q3 2026 on the ZARIN basis: 6.5% citywide average, 7.0% in Business Bay, 6.4% in Dubai Marina, 4.2% on Jumeirah Bay Island. The ROI predictor models it for any specific purchase.
Holiday Home Licence (DET)
The DET permit that lets a residential unit be let on short stays — nightly or weekly, through platforms. It is a business licence with its own rules; without it, short-term letting of a standard residence is not permitted.
Maintenance Responsibility
Major maintenance and structural repairs sit with the landlord unless the contract explicitly and lawfully says otherwise; the tenant handles day-to-day minor upkeep. Clauses that try to opt out of the law’s protections do not.
Net Yield
Gross yield minus the costs of holding — service charges, insurance, management, vacancy. The canonical example: a 1,000 sqft Marina unit at AED 1,850,000 renting near AED 118,400 is 6.4% gross but about 5.5% net once roughly AED 16,000 of service charges lands. The calculators run both lines.
Notice Period (90 Days)
Non-renewal requires 90 days’ written notice before the tenancy ends — from either side. Miss it and the tenancy typically rolls on at the capped increase.
Security Deposit (5% / 10%)
Five percent of the annual rent for an unfurnished letting, 10% furnished — held against damage and unpaid bills, returnable at checkout. It is not the final month’s rent; that misconception causes half the deposit disputes at the Rental Dispute Centre.
Sub-Letting Rules
A tenant may sub-let only with the landlord’s written consent; without it, sub-letting is a breach the landlord can act on. Short-term sub-lets additionally fall under holiday-home licensing.
Vacancy Rate
The share of the year a unit sits unlet — every vacant month costs about 8.3% of the annual rent. Which is why a reliable tenant at a fair rent beats a stretched asking price that sits empty.

Visas & Residency + Off-Plan

Residency routes through property and the mechanics of off-plan purchases: milestone payments, escrow protections, developer delivery and the visas they qualify for.

Construction Milestone
The defined build stages — foundation, superstructure, finishes — that release escrow funds to the developer and trigger your instalments. Your payment schedule is a mirror of someone else’s build calendar.
Down Payment (Booking Deposit)
The first instalment at reservation — commonly 10–20% on off-plan — paid into the project escrow account. Everything after it follows the milestone schedule, not the sales patter.
Emirates ID
The mandatory UAE identity card for residents: it opens bank accounts, DEWA contracts and Ejari registrations. Property-backed visa filings end with Emirates ID issuance — for the family, where sponsorship applies.
Entry Permit vs Residence Visa
The entry permit gets you in — single entry, short validity. The residence visa is the status that follows: issued, ID-linked and renewable. Property investor routes culminate in the residence visa, not the permit.
Escrow Release
The DLD-certified process by which escrowed buyer funds move to the developer — only against verified milestones, certified by the project engineer. It is the enforcement behind Law No. 8 of 2007, and a developer’s attitude to it is one of the signals in the developer reputation tracker.
Family Sponsorship
A resident sponsoring a spouse, children and — on conditions — parents onto their visa. The 10-year Golden Visa includes full family sponsorship; the two-year investor visa covers a spouse and children.
Golden Visa
The 10-year renewable residency granted from AED 2,000,000 in Dubai property — processing typically two to three months, full family sponsorship, and mortgaged property qualifies if the unpaid balance is no more than 50% of title deed value. The strongest residency instrument property buys; the Golden Visa page carries the filing detail.
Grace Period
The roughly 30 days after a residence visa is cancelled during which you may stay to settle affairs or depart without overstay fines. Plan the next filing — renewal, new sponsor, new property route — inside it.
Guaranteed Rental Return (GRR)
A developer’s promise of fixed rent for a set period, typically priced into the purchase price. Underwrite the yield on the open market — as the ROI predictor does — and treat the guarantee as upside, not arithmetic.
Investor Visa
The two-year renewable residency from AED 750,000 in property — the pragmatic route when the budget sits below the AED 2M Golden threshold. It keeps tenancy, banking and utility records continuous while the portfolio grows toward the upgrade; the investor visa page has the conditions.
Payment Plan
The instalment schedule of an off-plan purchase — a 60/40 plan, for example, collects 60% through construction milestones and 40% at handover. Structure changes price sensitivity, which is why the payment plan calculator exists.
Post-Handover Payment Plan
A plan that keeps collecting instalments for years after keys are delivered — effectively developer financing on the balance. Rent can service the instalments; model both together, honestly, before committing.
Remote Work Visa
The one-year renewable visa for employees working remotely for employers abroad — income-based, not property-based. Buyers often use it as the bridge while a Golden Visa filing runs its two-to-three-month course; the visa page carries the conditions.
Retirement Visa
The five-year renewable residency for applicants who meet the property and financial conditions of the route — property is one of the qualifying bases. It is the longest standard route after the Golden Visa; the retirement visa page carries the current conditions.
