Emirates
Hills

Emirates Hills is Dubai's definitive golf-estate address — 500+ freehold plots arranged around the 18-hole Montgomerie course, widely nicknamed the 'Beverly Hills of Dubai.' Emaar built the estate's infrastructure; every villa on it was custom-built by its owner, so no two homes — and no two transactions — are alike. Built mansions average AED 1,900 per sqft in 2026 and trade between AED 35M and AED 150M, with Hattat and Montgomerie frontages at the top of the band; the estate record stands at AED 280M (2022). Plots alone trade at AED 400-700 per sqft — the city's highest concentration of AED 50M+ transactions sits here. Five-year price CAGR of 9.8% and a 3.8% gross yield frame Emirates Hills as a wealth-preservation asset rather than an income play. Area Overview

Dubai's Beverly Hills, Explained Properly

The master development. Emaar developed Emirates Hills in the early 2000s as a serviced-plot estate: roads, gatehouses, landscaping, and the 18-hole Montgomerie championship course at the core — but no developer-built housing. More than 500 freehold plots were sold to individual owners, each of whom commissioned a private villa under ground-plus-one and ground-plus-two zoning on plots of 12,000 sqft and larger. Sectors E, L, and M carry the estate's best-known addresses, with Sector E holding the prime golf frontage. The estate's name and its neighbour — the Emirates Golf Club, home of the Desert Classic on the historic Majlis course — set the tone: this is Dubai's original golf-and-mansion community, adjacent to the Lakes, Meadows, and Springs villages that grew around it.

Who lives here. Emirates Hills has long been the address of Dubai's wealthiest families — owners who hold for decades, build staffed compounds behind mature walls, and treat the estate's privacy culture as part of the purchase. The character is low-rise by law: ground-plus-two zoning across the whole estate means no tower ever looks into a garden. The Address Montgomerie hotel sits inside the course; Dubai British School is adjacent, with Kings' School campuses minutes away — a school-run practicality that is genuinely unusual at this price point. Streets are quiet, patrol-covered, and deliberately uncommercial: no retail inside the estate, no through-roads past the prime frontages. Daily life runs on the estate's own geography: morning rounds on the course, the Address Montgomerie's terraces for meetings, and the Meadows and Lakes villages' retail — supermarkets, clinics, mosques — five minutes from every gate. Weekend traffic is golf carts and school-friend visits; the estate has no retail of its own, and residents prefer it that way.

The wealth thesis. Emirates Hills is a land market wearing a villa market's clothes. Plots trade at AED 400-700 per sqft of land — interior sectors at the bottom of the band, golf frontage at the top — and on prime plots the land can represent half or more of the transaction. Five-year price CAGR of 9.8% is the highest of Dubai's established villa communities, service charges of AED 8 per sqft per year are the lowest in the city for a villa estate of this scale, and the estate concentrates more AED 50M+ transactions than any other Dubai address — the record standing at AED 280M (2022). Compared with the city's newer branded villa districts, the arithmetic is stark: land appreciates, buildings depreciate, and Emirates Hills is the one Dubai estate where buyers consistently price that difference correctly. At a 3.8% gross yield, nobody buys here for income; they buy for the land, the frontage, and the fact that no two estates — or sellers — are alike.

Getting Around

Emirates Hills sits between two Sheikh Zayed Road interchanges, which is the whole of its connectivity story: Dubai Marina is 12 minutes, Mall of the Emirates 10, and DXB about 15 via Sheikh Zayed Road in off-peak conditions; Downtown and DIFC run 18-20 minutes. The estate's school adjacency is unusual at this price point — Dubai British School sits at the community's edge, with Kings' School campuses 6-9 minutes away — and the Lakes, Meadows, and Springs communities surrounding the estate supply retail, clinics, and mosques without crossing a highway. There is no metro within the estate; the nearest Red Line stations are a 10-12 minute drive, and households run chauffeured or self-driven vehicles through the community gatehouses.

Investment Analysis

A Land Market Wearing a Villa Market's Clothes

Emirates Hills is a land market wearing a villa market's clothes. The 500+ plots that Emaar serviced in the early 2000s are the real asset; the mansions on them are, in financial terms, improvements of varying quality. Understanding that distinction — and pricing land and built value separately — is the entire discipline of buying well here.

Yield & Wealth Preservation

At 3.8% gross, Emirates Hills is the lowest-yielding of Dubai's major villa estates — and its owners are generally unmoved. The asset's job is preservation: five-year price CAGR of 9.8% is the highest of Dubai's established villa communities, service charges of AED 8 per sqft per year are the lowest in the city for a villa estate of this scale, and the estate concentrates more AED 50M+ transactions than any other Dubai address. The 2022 record — an AED 280M golf-frontage mansion sale — remains one of the largest residential transactions in the city's history.

Market History 2020–2026

Through 2020, Emirates Hills plots traded near AED 300-400 per sqft of land. The 2021-2023 villa boom more than doubled land values: Sector E golf-frontage plots cleared AED 700/sqft, and the AED 280M record sale in 2022 re-priced the entire estate. 2024-2026 brought consolidation — fewer transactions, firmer asking prices, and a widening spread between Sector E frontage and interior plots. By 2026, land trades at AED 400-700 per sqft of plot and built mansions at AED 1,900 per sqft of built-up area on average, with the market spanning AED 35M to AED 150M.

Outlook 2026–2028

No new plots can be added; the only supply is resale and rebuild. That rebuild cycle is the quiet story of 2026-2028: original early-2000s villas on prime plots are being acquired at near-land value, demolished, and replaced by contemporary mansions — a value-add path of AED 15M-40M per plot for buyers willing to run a 24-36 month build. ZARIN's base case is 7-10% annual growth on land through 2028, frontage-dependent. The risks: estate values track global wealth cycles closely, and single-asset concentration cuts both ways.

Emirates Hills FAQ

Eight Questions, Answered Properly

The questions ZARIN advisors hear most often about Emirates Hills. If yours isn't covered, message us on WhatsApp for a direct answer — typically within an hour during business hours.

What is the entry price for Emirates Hills?
Plots enter at AED 15M; built villas from AED 35M — typically an original early-2000s build on a larger interior plot, priced near land value. Contemporary mansions run AED 45M-60M in Sectors L and M, Montgomerie-facing estates AED 60M-100M, and Sector E golf-frontage mansions AED 80M-150M. The estate record stands at AED 280M (2022). Land trades at AED 400-700 per sqft of plot; built-up values average AED 1,900 per sqft across the estate. ZARIN values every property here twice — as land and as built product — before advising.

Should I buy a plot or a built mansion in Emirates Hills?
Both paths work; they optimise for different things. Buying a plot at AED 400-700 per sqft of land and building lets you control orientation, specification, and architecture — with construction at roughly AED 3,000-4,000 per sqft of built-up area and a 24-36 month programme, the all-in cost typically lands 10-20% below an equivalent finished mansion. Buying built means walking into a finished estate today at AED 1,900 per sqft average BUA, with the premium going to contemporary Sector E product. The deciding factors are timeline tolerance and architecture — and note that demolishing an older mansion to build your own is a real strategy here, cheaper than it sounds when the land carries the value.

What is the process to build my own villa in Emirates Hills?
The sequence is well-trodden. Purchase the plot (freehold, DLD title), appoint an architect, and submit the design through the community's design review and the Dubai Land Department NOC process. Zoning allows ground-plus-one and ground-plus-two heights on plots of 12,000 sqft or more; most owners build 15,000-30,000 sqft of built-up area. Construction runs 24-36 months with independent engineering supervision. ZARIN manages the full path — plot selection with buildability analysis, design-team introduction, approvals, and handover — and our founder has run Dubai land and build projects since 2005.

Do Emirates Hills residents get golf membership?
Membership of the Montgomerie Dubai — the 18-hole championship course at the estate's core, operated alongside the Address Montgomerie hotel — is purchased through the club, not bundled with title. Several membership tiers exist, with resident waitlists typically shorter than the city norm. The neighbouring Emirates Golf Club, home of the Desert Classic on the Majlis course, is a five-minute drive and the origin of the estate's name. Many owners hold memberships at both; the golf is genuinely why some sectors command what they do. ZARIN facilitates introductions as part of an acquisition.

Do famous residents live in Emirates Hills? How is privacy handled?
The estate has long been home to many of Dubai's most prominent families — and we will not be naming any of them. Privacy here is structural: high walls and mature landscaping, ground-plus-two zoning that keeps the estate low-rise, a 24/7 community patrol, and a layout with no through-roads past the prime frontages. ZARIN operates under a formal discretion protocol for estate transactions: NDA-first mandates, off-market launches where sellers prefer, and viewings arranged privately with owner consent. What we can say: the estate's nickname — the 'Beverly Hills of Dubai' — understates the discretion.

What are the service charges in Emirates Hills?
AED 8 per sqft per year — the lowest service-charge level of any Dubai villa estate of this scale. The community fee covers roads, street lighting, common landscaping, the 24/7 security patrol, and gatehouse operations; everything inside your wall — pool, garden, systems — is privately maintained. On a 20,000 sqft plot, the community charge runs roughly AED 160,000 per year. This is one of the quiet economic arguments for the estate: the holding cost of an AED 60M mansion here is a fraction of an equivalent branded villa district's.

Emirates Hills vs Jumeirah Bay Island — how do they compare?
Both are wealth-preservation markets; they differ in what the wealth sits on. Emirates Hills is a land market: larger plots (12,000-43,000+ sqft), golf frontage, ground-plus-two mansions, AED 1,900 per sqft average BUA, 9.8% five-year CAGR, and a 3.8% yield. Jumeirah Bay is an island market: 128 plots, sea frontage, single-bridge privacy, AED 4,200 per sqft BUA, 8.5% CAGR, and a 4.2% yield. Emirates Hills offers more entry points (plots from AED 15M) and a self-build value path; Jumeirah Bay offers structural privacy and beach frontage that cannot be replicated. Many of our clients ultimately hold both.

How does the Emirates Hills resale market work?
Slowly, privately, and at the top of the market. The estate concentrates more AED 50M+ transactions than any other Dubai address, yet quality listings are scarce: owners hold for decades, and much of the flow begins off-market through advisor-to-advisor contact. Price discovery uses two references — comparable transactions by sector and frontage, and the land-value floor (AED 400-700 per sqft of plot) beneath every property. Well-priced mansions move in 3-9 months; aspirational pricing can sit for years without a single viewing. ZARIN's Private Office maintains the estate's off-market register and buyer list.
